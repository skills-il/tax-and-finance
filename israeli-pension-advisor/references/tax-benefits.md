# Pension Tax Benefits in Israel

## Employee Tax Benefits

### Tax Credit (Zikui Mas)
- 35% tax credit on employee pension contributions
- Ceiling: 8,148 NIS/year (679 NIS/month, 2026); the 679 figure equals 7% of qualifying salary 9,700 NIS
- Credit directly reduces tax liability
- Example: qualifying salary 9,700 NIS/month × 7% = 679 NIS/month contribution. Tax credit: 679 × 35% = 238 NIS/month tax reduction (~2,852 NIS/year)
- No Section 47 deduction for an employee's deposits from insured salary. Extra credit or deduction arises only on non-insured pay components (up to 485 NIS/month extra deposit in 2026 per Kol Zchut); separately, loss-of-work-capacity premiums are deductible up to 3.5% of salary (s.32(14)(b))

### Tax Deduction (Nikui Mas)
- Employer's tagmulim contribution is not taxed as income for the employee up to 7.5% of salary, with salary capped at 34,423 NIS/month (2.5x the average wage, s.3(e3))
- Employer severance deposits are exempt up to the lower of one month's salary or 45,600 NIS per year (2026)
- Reduces taxable income, not tax directly
- Benefit depends on marginal tax rate

## Self-Employed Tax Benefits

### Mandatory Pension Obligation (2016 Law)

#### Contribution Rates
- 4.45% on net taxable income up to 6,884.50 NIS/month (half average wage)
- 12.55% on income from 6,884.50 to 13,769 NIS/month (full average wage)
- No obligation on income above full average wage
- Maximum annual mandatory obligation: ~14,044 NIS
- Statute: Economic Efficiency Law 5777-2016, Chapter B (in force 01.01.2017)
- Obligation applies from age 21 until early-retirement age 60, both tested at the end of the tax year; anyone aged 55+ on 01.01.2017 is exempt
- New-business exemption: fewer than 6 months since first VAT registration as an osek, measured at the end of the tax year
- Calculated on net taxable income (after deductions), not gross revenue

#### Tax Benefit Rates on Voluntary Pension Contributions
- First 5.5% of qualifying income: Tax credit at 35% (up to 12,804 NIS/year). The last 0.5% applies only if no loss-of-work-capacity premiums were deducted under s.32(14)(b); otherwise the credit is 5% (up to 11,640) instead of 5.5%
- Additional 11% of qualifying income: Tax deduction (up to 25,608 NIS/year)
- Maximum deductible pension contribution: 38,412 NIS/year (16.5% of income up to 232,800 NIS)
- These tiers apply to a beneficiary member (amit mutav): kitzbah deposits in the year of at least 16% of the annual average wage (26,436 NIS in 2026). A non-beneficiary member gets lower limits under Sections 45a(d) and 47(b); check the Tax Authority's current guidance before quoting them
- Qualifying income ceiling for pension: 232,800 NIS/year (19,400 NIS/month, 2026)

### Keren Hishtalmut
- Tax deduction: up to 13,203 NIS/year (4.5% of income up to 293,397 NIS)
- Profit-exempt ceiling: 20,566 NIS/year
- Gains tax-free after 6 years

## Universal Benefits

### Keren Hishtalmut Gains
- After 6-year lock-in: gains on the tax-favoured deposits are tax-free; gains on deposits above the ceiling are not
- No capital gains tax on withdrawal
- This is unique to the Israeli tax system
- For employees: both employee and employer portions, up to the salary cap
- 2024 Treasury proposal to limit this benefit has not been enacted as of May 2026; monitor for renewed legislative activity

### Pension Payout Tax Exemption (Tikun 190 schedule)
- Monthly pension partially tax-exempt under Amendment 190 to the Income Tax Ordinance (2012), with the schedule re-split by Amendment 275 (December 2024)
- 2026 exemption rate: 57.5% (rising to 62.5% in 2027, 67% from 2028)
- Tax-free amount: up to 5,422 NIS/month pension income (2026)
- Qualifying pension threshold (kitzbah mezakah): 9,430 NIS/month
- Tax-exempt severance ceiling: 13,750 NIS per year of service (2026)

### Tikun 190 Deposits (post-60 tax shelter)
The other half of Amendment 190: deposits with no employer match and no credit or deduction claimed become a "recognised pension" (kitzbah mukeret), exempt when received from age 60.

- **No pension condition at deposit.** The minimum pension (~5,306 NIS/month in 2026) is tested when a lump sum is taken. Only kitzbah-fund and budget pensions count; Bituach Leumi old-age and survivors' pensions do NOT
- **First 38,412 NIS deposited in a tax year (2026)** is ordinary qualifying-pension money; only the excess is a recognised pension. No cap on the excess
- **Withdrawal as monthly kitzbah from age 60:** Fully exempt from income tax for life
- **Withdrawal as lump sum:** 15% tax on the NOMINAL GAIN only; principal not taxed. The base is nominal, never real (CPI-adjusted)
- **Standard use cases:** severance at retirement, sale of business proceeds, retirement bonus, inheritance, excess liquid savings
- **Comparison:** the 15% applies to the NOMINAL gain, and the benchmark it is measured against is 25% on the REAL (CPI-adjusted) gain in a regular investment account, not the marginal rate. A 1M NIS deposit drawn as a lump sum after 10 years with a 500K nominal gain pays 15% x 500K = 75K NIS. Compare that against 25% of the real gain over the same period before calling it a saving: when inflation has been high, the real gain is much smaller than the nominal one and the nominal-basis 15% can be the worse of the two.
- **Estate planning:** Funds pass to designated beneficiaries under fund rules. Death before 75: tax-free if withdrawn within 3 months, then 25% on the real gain accruing after that. Death after 75 before the pension began: treated as commuted (15% on the gain); a beneficiary aged 60+ may take it as an exempt pension
- **PFIC caveat:** US persons should consult a tax professional; kupot gemel may be PFICs under US tax law

### Heichum Kitzbah (the other use of "Tikun 190")
- The same 2012 amendment introduced the heichum kitzbah mechanism that links severance withdrawal to the future kitzbah-mezakah exemption pool
- Each shekel of tax-exempt severance withdrawn within 32 years before the eligibility age (the later of retirement age and the age the qualifying pension starts) reduces the lifetime kitzbah-mezakah pool by the same amount multiplied by a coefficient of 1.35
- Lifetime tax-exempt capital pool (kibua zechuyot) = 180 × 57.5% × 9,430 = 976,005 NIS for a 2026 retiree (grows with the exemption rate toward 67% by 2028)
- Withdrawals outside the 32-year window have no impact

## Section 14 (Severance Arrangement, Severance Pay Law 1963)
- Employer ongoing contributions at 8.33% of salary satisfy full severance obligation
- Employee keeps accumulated pension even if terminated
- Employer released from additional severance payment in the fully-funded sub-case only
**Section 14 (4 real-world sub-cases, per the אישור כללי of 30 June 1998 and amendments):**
1. Full 8.33% from day 1 on the full salary -- the textbook case; employer fully released from supplemental severance.
2. Section 14 on part of salary only (e.g. base, not bonus); the unfunded portion stays under the general law, calculated at final salary on termination.
3. Section 14 starting after a delay (no pension for the first months); employer owes the gap for the pre-Section-14 period at FINAL salary times years, indexed -- often the largest source of שלמת פיצויים claims.
4. Collective-agreement Section 14 (industry or Histadrut); bonuses, retroactive raises, and certain allowances may sit outside the arrangement and trigger residual severance liability.

- Mandatory pension law requires only 6% for severance component; the 2.33% gap (8.33% - 6%) is the employer's exposure in the partially-funded sub-cases, calculated at final salary
- Under the General Approval, if the employer does not pay the additional 2 1/3% on top of the pension contributions, the payments replace only 72% of the employee's severance; the rest can be owed on dismissal

## Commonly Overlooked Benefits
Facts, not recommendations; which to use is a question for a licensed pension adviser.
- Keren hishtalmut gains are tax-free after 6 years, within the ceilings above.
- The employee credit and the self-employed credit and deduction ceilings are listed above.
- Management fees have two components (deposits and balance); small differences compound.
- Tikun 190 deposits: see above.
- Heichum kitzbah does not apply to severance withdrawn more than 32 years before the eligibility age.

### Options at termination (Form 161 Part B)
No option is ranked here; which one fits a given user is a question for a licensed pension adviser.
- **Rezef zechuyot (continuity):** leave severance in the kupah, no tax event, no impact on the future exemption pool. Works cleanly only if the new employer's severance contributions land in a compatible continuity arrangement; otherwise the severance can sit frozen in the old fund.
- **Cash withdrawal:** exempt up to 13,750 NIS per year of service; within 32 years of the eligibility age each exempt shekel reduces the pool by 1.35.
- **Reverting a rezef election later (Form 161ג):** the form is a request to revert from rezef-pitzuyim or rezef-kitzbah. Reverting rezef-pitzuyim is limited to 2 years from termination and has further conditions; reverting rezef-kitzbah is not time-limited. It is NOT a route for returning severance already withdrawn; this skill has no verified procedure for that, so send the user to the Tax Authority or a licensed adviser.
- **Prisat pituyim (spreading):** spread the taxable portion (above the exemption) across up to 6 tax years to lower the marginal rate; rule of thumb, 1 spread-year per 4 years of seniority. Backward spreading exists but is rare and needs specific approval.
