### Cashback Stacking Strategy

Maximize savings by combining multiple discount layers on a single purchase:

1. **Credit card cashback** -- use a card with cashback for the store category (a flat-cashback card for general spending, or a card whose club benefit covers that specific chain)
2. **Cashback platform** -- route purchase through Cashback.co.il or Cashdo for additional rebate
3. **Store loyalty program** -- earn points at the retailer's own club
4. **Discounted gift card** -- buy BuyMe or store gift cards at a discount, pay with them
5. **Coupon codes** -- check Israeli coupon aggregators before checkout

**Example (hypothetical rates, for the arithmetic only):** a 1,000 NIS appliance, paid with a BuyMe card bought at 10% off through a welfare club, so the user pays 900 NIS for the card:
- Discounted gift card: 100 NIS
- Credit card 3% on the 900 NIS card purchase: 27 NIS, only if the card's cashback covers gift-card purchases (check the terms; many programmes exclude them)
- Cashback platform 5%: in general experience usually 0, because platforms commonly exclude orders paid with a gift card or store credit. Where one does track the order, it rebates on the order value the store sees (here 1,000 NIS, often before VAT and shipping), not on the 900 NIS paid for the card
- Realistic total: 100 + 27 = 127 NIS (12.7%), or 100 NIS if the card excludes gift-card purchases

PayBack belongs to Max, so with a Max card check the PayBack terms before assuming the two rebates stack.

### Key Credit Card Benefits to Use

- **Installment payments (tashlumim)** -- many Israeli retailers offer interest-free installments, but in general experience the interest-free window is often short; longer terms (and credit-company "credit"/kredit plans) frequently carry interest. Always verify "tashlumim l'lo ribit" (interest-free) for the specific term before choosing installments over a single payment
- **Extended warranty and purchase protection** -- some premium cards include them, but the length and coverage differ from card to card, so read the specific card's terms before relying on either
- **Airport lounge access** -- premium cards include Priority Pass or equivalent. Verify on the specific card's terms; lounge benefits are cut and re-added frequently
- **Insurance benefits** -- travel insurance, purchase insurance, extended warranty

## Subscription Audit Workflow

### Step 1: Identify All Recurring Charges

Check these sources for recurring charges:
- **Credit card statements** -- log into Cal, Max, or Isracard portal, filter by recurring/hora'at keva
- **Bank standing orders (hora'ot keva)** -- check your bank app for automatic debits
- **App Store / Google Play** -- check subscription settings for in-app subscriptions
- **Email search** -- search inbox for "renewal", "subscription", "charged", "tashlum"
- **Annual charges statement** -- every continuous-deal business must send, each March, a statement of last year's charges and any unpaid balance (s.13ב(ב1)); ask for it if it did not arrive. A debt beyond it is the business's to prove

### Step 2: Categorize and Evaluate

| Category | Common Israeli Services | Indicative Monthly Cost | Alternatives |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|-------------|
| Streaming (video) | HOT VOD, YES+, Netflix, Disney+, Apple TV+ | 30-50 NIS each | Share family plans, rotate services |
| Streaming (music) | Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Premium | 20-35 NIS | Family plan (up to 6 users) |
| Telecom | Cellcom, Partner, Pelephone, HOT Mobile | 50-150 NIS | Move to a budget plan (Golan, Rami Levy Mobile). Port the number, do not cancel first, see Gotcha 9 |
| Internet | Bezeq, HOT, Partner, Cellcom | 80-150 NIS | Bundle with TV, negotiate renewal |
| Gym | Holmes Place, Hapoel, local studios | 150-400 NIS | Municipal pools/gyms (much cheaper). Gym contracts have their own statutory exit, see Gotcha 6 |
| Electricity | IEC plus private suppliers | Varies with consumption | A private supplier sells the same electricity at a discount off the regulated tariff, see Gotcha 10 |
| Insurance | Various | 100-500 NIS | Compare annually; pull your full policy list from Har HaBituach (harb.cma.gov.il) to spot duplicate coverage|
| Bank fees | All banks | 20-50 NIS | Negotiate or switch to a digital bank, and see Gotcha 7 on the Bank of Israel fee reform |
| Cloud storage | iCloud, Google One, Dropbox | 10-30 NIS | Use free tier, consolidate services |
| News | Haaretz, TheMarker, Ynet+ | 30-60 NIS | Check if included with credit card perks |

### Step 3: Take Action

For each subscription, decide:

| Decision | Action | Indicative Savings |
|----------|--------|------------------|
| **Cancel** | Unused or rarely used service | Full monthly cost |
| **Downgrade** | Premium plan but only use basic features | 30-50% of cost |
| **Switch** | Cheaper alternative exists | 20-60% of cost |
| **Share** | Split family/group plan | 50-80% of cost |
| **Negotiate** | Call retention dept, threaten to cancel | 10-30% discount |
| **Keep** | Good value, actively used | No change |

### Step 4: Calculate Annual Savings

Add up monthly savings x 12 where prices are flat. Even 200 NIS/month in savings = **2,400 NIS/year**. Where a price changes mid-year (introductory months, a promotion ending) or an exit costs a notice month and a fee (a gym), sum the year month by month instead.

## Gotchas

### 1. Cashback Platform Conflicts
Some cashback platforms (Cashback.co.il, Cashdo) do not stack with each other. Using two cashback extensions simultaneously can cause neither to track the purchase. Always use only one cashback platform per transaction.

### 2. Whether a Gift Card Can Be Refunded Depends on Where It Was Bought
**A card bought online** is a distance sale: it can be cancelled within 14 days (s.14ג(ג)), the business must refund within 14 days and may keep at most **5% of the price or 100 NIS, whichever is lower** (s.14ה(ב)(1)), and a person aged 65 or over, a person with a disability, or a new immigrant within five years has **four months** where the deal involved a conversation with the business (s.14ג1). **A card bought in a shop**: whether it can be returned depends on the shop's posted returns policy (s.4ג), so do not tell the user flatly that it is non-refundable.

**Validity.** Under s.14ח(ב) a general gift card that carries an expiry date must run **at least five years** from issuance, but the s.14ח(א) definition excludes a card meant for one type of product or service only (a spa day, say). The Payment Services (Exemption) Regulations add a renewal right (a further 5 years, or 10 years where the balance exceeds 150 NIS) and yearly notices, but **only** for exempt multi-merchant cards (not reloadable, holding up to 1,500 NIS) issued from 14 April 2022, and the regulations themselves run only until 14 July 2028. So "up to 15 years" is a floor plus a conditional renewal, not a single validity. Check the conditions in `references/consumer-law-details.md` before telling a user a card is dead, or that it can be renewed.

### 3. Installment Payments (Tashlumim) Are Not Always Free
Israeli retailers advertise installments freely, but the interest-free window is set per retailer and per product, and in general experience is often only a few payments. Longer plans, and the credit-card companies' own "kredit" plans, frequently carry interest. There is no general rule that a particular number of payments is safe, so read the specific offer and confirm "tashlumim l'lo ribit" for the exact number of payments before choosing installments over a single charge.

### 4. Promotional and Retention Prices End, and the Law Says How
A discounted period given on terms (a 12-month promotional price, a retention discount for staying) is generally a "עסקה לתקופה קצובה" under **section 13א**; the definition excludes a price cut for a limited period without any condition. Where the business charges by standing authorisation, it must give the end date between 60 and 30 days before it, print it on every invoice in the last three months, and send an SMS 21 days before if it has the user's mobile number. By default a term continuing the deal past the end date has no effect (13א(ג)(1)). The price may rise only under one of two independent exceptions (13א(ד)):

- **Telephony or gas** (the Third Schedule), with a written notice in the notice period stating the terms and the right to end the deal at any time. Home internet and pay TV are **not** in that Schedule.
- **Any discount-period deal, a telephony one included**, where the post-promotion amounts were stated in writing at signing and a written notice was given. No notice is needed where the discount ends within four months of signing (introductory months), but the consumer may then leave after that date free of charge.

If the business keeps charging after the user asked it **in writing** to stop, or without the required written notice, s.31א(א)(2א) allows statutory damages up to 10,000 NIS without proof of loss; the written request (email counts) is a precondition to suing (s.31א(ב)). Diarise the end date. Detail: `references/consumer-law-details.md`.

### 5. Israeli Consumer Protection Law on Cancellation
The 14-day cooling-off right ("tkufat tzinun") attaches to door-to-door sales (Sec. 14) and distance/online sales (Sec. 14ג), not to every contract. A continuous service signed up online or by phone is also a distance sale, so within 14 days it can be cancelled with a s.14ה refund whether or not the service has begun (s.14ג(ג)(2)). At any time, continuous contracts (telecom, streaming, and similar) can be ended under the continuous-transaction (esek mitmashech) provisions, which are unusually favourable:

- **Section 14ט(א)** requires the business to accept a cancellation notice by **any** of: orally by phone or at the premises, registered mail, email, fax (if it has one), the internet where the deal could be made online, and any further channel the Minister sets.
- **Section 14ט(ב)**: where the deal can be made online, the business must place a **dedicated cancellation link on the home page of its website**, positioned "באופן מובלט וברור" (prominently and clearly). This is a duty, not a courtesy, so a user who cannot find it is not stuck with a phone queue.
- **Section 13ד(ג)**: the contract ends within **three business days** of the notice, or **six business days** if sent by registered mail, and from that date the business must stop supply and stop charging for anything supplied afterwards.
- **Section 31א** lets a court award **statutory damages up to 10,000 NIS with no proof of loss**, including where the consumer sent a cancellation notice under 14ט and the business kept charging (para (2ב)), and where an overcharge was not refunded (para (2ב1)). A claim requires a prior **written** request; for (2ב) a 14ט notice suffices, but **not an oral one** (s.31א(ב)). So cancel by email, the website link or registered mail, and keep the proof.
- **Section 18ב**: telephony, mobile, internet access, cable and satellite TV, gas, the essential-service electricity supplier and water suppliers (the Second Schedule) must run a free phone line, and where it uses automatic call routing, a professional human must answer a cancellation ("סיום התקשרות") call within **six minutes**, with no forced voicemail (a sector regulator may approve deviations).

### 6. Gym and Timeshare Contracts Have Their Own Exit Route
A fixed-term gym membership is cancellable at any time under **section 13א1 and the Fourth Schedule**: notice **in writing**, effective **at the end of one month**. If use has begun, the business may keep the pro-rata value of use up to the effective date and a fee, which is **not** a flat percentage: months from the start up to the effective date (the notice month included) multiplied by the gap between the contract's average monthly price and the monthly price of the alternative open-ended track, **and only where that alternative price was disclosed in writing before signing**. The fee is capped at **the lower of** 25% of the consideration (registration fees included) in the first third of the term, 20% in the second, 17% in the last, and what remains to be paid. The definition of מכון כושר excludes some courses and seasonal pools, and the same schedule covers a timeshare (יחידת נופש) on its own terms; see `references/consumer-law-details.md`. Ask for the calculation, and do not let a user be told the contract is "locked for a year".

### 7. Bank Fee Reform: the Debit-Card Cap is Live, the Rest is Staged
The Bank of Israel published a reform of household and small-business account fees on 21 June 2026 (circular ח-06-2851). Check the date:

- **From 1 October 2026 (in force)**: the monthly immediate-debit card fee is capped at **7 NIS**, for every card including ones issued earlier. The existing three-year debit-fee exemption for a customer who also holds a credit card continues **in parallel until 1 July 2027**, so a user on that exemption may stay exempt until then, though banks may bring the change forward. From the same date banks may no longer charge for a cheque returned for a technical reason, for locating accounts, or for handling inheritances and estates.
- **From 1 July 2027** (banks may start earlier): the new payment-account service, capping the monthly fee at **10 NIS for the first 100 transactions**, 5 NIS where 0 to 2 transactions were made, and 1 NIS for each transaction beyond 100.

It applies automatically and is addressed to banking corporations, so do not assume it reaches credit-card company fees. Tell the user to check the October statement: a bank debit-card fee above 7 NIS is a question for the bank and then for the Bank of Israel's public enquiries unit (היחידה לפניות הציבור ולבקרה צרכנית), not for the Consumer Council. Do not tell a user the account-fee caps already apply before July 2027.

### 8. A Refund Voucher is Not the Same as a Gift Card
Where a purchase is cancelled and the business hands out a credit voucher (שובר זיכוי) instead of money, **section 14ז** sets its own rules: it is valid for **at least two years** from the day it is handed over, it must be for the amount actually paid, redemption may **not** be conditioned on producing the original invoice, and a term restricting redemption (including during a sale) has no effect. And under 14ז(ג), if the user buys something cheaper than the voucher's face value and the leftover is **5% of the voucher or 100 NIS, whichever is lower**, they are entitled to that leftover **in cash**. This applies to a gift voucher too.

### 9. Which Cancellation Regime Applies, and the Telecom Trap

Four regimes sit side by side and they are not interchangeable. Help the user identify which regime the contract appears to fall under, and have them confirm it against the contract, before drafting anything:

| The contract | Route | Key mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous service (streaming, cloud, most subscriptions) | s.14ט notice + s.13ד(ג) | Any of the six channels; ends in 3 business days, 6 by registered mail |
| Gym / health-club membership for a fixed term | s.13א1 + Fourth Schedule | Written notice; effective at the end of one month; capped cancellation fee |
| One-off purchase made online or by phone | s.14ג + s.14ה | 14 days to cancel; a fee of at most 5% of the price or 100 NIS, whichever is lower, and **no fee** where cancelling for a defect, a mismatch, late delivery or another breach (s.14ה(א)) |
| A credit voucher received instead of a refund | s.14ז | 2 years' validity, no invoice needed, cash change on a small remainder |

**Mobile lines are the exception, and getting it wrong is expensive.** Do NOT send a cancellation notice to the current mobile carrier as the first step. Start the move at the **receiving** provider and let the number-porting process close the old line; a cancellation sent first can release the number, and the user loses the number itself, not just the discount. Cancel first only where the user genuinely wants the line gone.

**When the merchant stonewalls, there is a second lever.** A recurring charge taken by standing authorisation (הרשאה לחיוב חשבון) can also be stopped through the bank, independently of the merchant, so a user who cannot get a straight answer should raise it with the bank as well as sending the statutory notice. That is a parallel route, not a substitute for the notice, because the s.31א remedy depends on the notice.

### 10. Electricity Supply is a Switchable Contract Too
Household electricity supply in Israel is open to private suppliers who sell at a discount off the regulated tariff while the physical supply and the grid stay unchanged. It belongs in the subscription audit because the saving is annual and applies to a bill nobody thinks of as a subscription. Suppliers offer two kinds of track: a flat discount at all hours (Cellcom Energy, for example, publishes 7.5% Sunday to Thursday) and time-of-use tracks with larger discounts in set hours (the same page offers 15% from 23:00 to 07:00). A time-of-use track only saves money if consumption actually falls in those hours, and the user should ask whether their meter type qualifies for it. Ask the supplier whether an existing IEC discount entitlement carries over. Treat it like any other contract: check the discount actually offered, the term, and what happens when a promotional period ends, and compare against the regulated tariff rather than against the supplier's own headline.

## Troubleshooting

### Error: "Cashback not tracked"
Cause: Ad blocker or competing browser extension interfered with tracking
Solution: Disable ad blockers and other cashback extensions before clicking through. Use incognito mode with only the cashback extension enabled. Contact cashback platform support with order confirmation for manual credit.

### Error: "Cannot cancel subscription"
Cause: Service provider making cancellation difficult
Solution: section 14ט(א) of the Consumer Protection Law obliges the business to accept a cancellation notice through several channels, including email and (where the deal could be made online) the internet, and 14ט(ב) obliges it to put a prominent cancellation link on its website's home page. Send the notice in writing through one of those channels (an oral notice does not support a later s.31א claim) and keep the proof; under 13ד(ג) the contract then ends within three business days, six if sent by registered mail. If charges continue after that, section 31א allows statutory damages of up to 10,000 NIS without proof of loss. Escalate to the Consumer Council (Moetzet HaTzarchanut) or the Gov.il complaint service.

### Error: "I was charged more than the agreed price on a recurring subscription"
Cause: A price rise at renewal, a discount that expired, or a billing error on the standing authorisation
Solution: Section 13ד1 gives a concrete timetable. Once the consumer says they were charged an amount above what the terms allow, the business has **ten business days** to investigate and **twenty-one business days** to send a reasoned written answer. If it finds it overcharged, it must refund within **four business days**, with shekel interest from the date of collection plus 16 NIS towards the consumer's expenses. Both that 16 NIS and the 50 NIS threshold below are the statute's base amounts, updated by the CPI every 1 January since 2013 (13ד1(ג)), so the current figures are somewhat higher. A refund under 50 NIS may instead be credited on the next billing date. If the refund never comes, section 31א(א)(2ב1) allows statutory damages of up to 10,000 NIS without proof of loss.

### Error: "Credit card benefits not applied"
Cause: Purchase category may not match the cashback category
Solution: Check your credit card's specific benefit terms. Some cards define categories narrowly (e.g., "supermarkets" may not include convenience stores). Contact the card company to verify which merchant codes qualify.
