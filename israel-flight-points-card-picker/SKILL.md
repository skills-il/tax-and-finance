### Step 4: Calculate estimated annual points

Use this formula per card:

    Annual points = (Monthly eligible spend) x 12 x (card earn rate)
    Eligible spend = Total monthly spend minus excluded-sector spend

Present a simple table with two or three spend scenarios (e.g., NIS 3,000 / NIS 6,000 / NIS 10,000 per month eligible).

### Step 5: Recommend and summarize

Based on the user profile from Step 1, recommend one primary card and one alternative. State:
- Why the primary card fits their spend profile
- What the annual fee payback period looks like
- Any bank account requirement the user needs to meet
- A reminder to read the current terms before applying (point values and rules change)

## Gotchas

- **Point devaluation risk:** Israeli travel card issuers have historically changed earn rates and redemption values with little advance notice. Any calculation this skill produces reflects conditions at a point in time. Always tell the user to confirm the current rate before applying.
- **Excluded sectors are per-card:** The exclusion list provided is a common baseline, but each card's terms may have slightly different wording or additional excluded MCC (merchant category codes). The Flycard terms PDF is the most detailed source; cite it when precision matters.
- **Toll roads use a separate charge account:** Kvish 6 and Carmel Tunnels bills are charged via a dedicated payment account (not directly on the credit card transaction), so they typically do not appear as credit card purchases at all. Users may not realize this is already excluded.
- **Foreign currency transactions:** Purchases in foreign currency (USD, EUR, etc.) often earn at a different rate than NIS transactions. Some cards offer bonus points for abroad spending. This can significantly affect the recommendation for travelers.
- **Annual fee waivers:** Some cards waive the annual fee in the first year, or waive it conditionally (e.g., minimum annual spend). Confirm the waiver condition before quoting net value.
- **Bank account requirement:** Beyond (FIBI) requires a FIBI account. Fly All (Discount) may require or prefer a Discount Bank account. Flycard and CAL Fly All are more bank-agnostic. Flag this to the user early.
- **VAT on annual fees:** The annual fee is typically quoted including VAT (currently 18%). If the user asks about cost, confirm whether quoted fees are gross or net.
- **BiDi note:** Card names like "Flycard", "Fly All", and "Beyond" are Latin-script brand names that appear inside Hebrew sentences. In WhatsApp or some email clients the visual order of mixed Hebrew-Latin lines may look reversed. When drafting Hebrew output, place the brand name at the start or end of a sentence to avoid ambiguity.

## Troubleshooting

### The source URL returns a 404 or the page has changed

Cause: Israeli bank and credit card company websites reorganize pages frequently, and PDF links to terms documents change when terms are updated.
Solution: Do not fabricate figures. Tell the user: "The page I have on record may have moved. Please search [issuer name] + 'כרטיס נקודות תנאים' on the issuer's website, or call the card company directly at the number on the back of the card." For Isracard, the main domain is isracard.co.il; for CAL, cal-online.co.il; for FIBI, fibi.co.il; for Discount, discountbank.co.il.

### User's monthly spend is mostly in excluded sectors

Cause: Some users have high fixed costs (arnona, electricity, insurance) that dominate their budget and earn zero points.
Solution: Recalculate eligible spend after stripping all excluded categories. If eligible spend drops below roughly NIS 2,000-3,000/month, point out that the annual fee may not be recovered by points alone and suggest the user also consider a cashback card as a baseline (note: cashback comparison is outside this skill's scope, but the flag is important).

### User asks which card is best for buying forex or abroad

Cause: Foreign currency earn rates are often different from domestic rates and may also include a foreign transaction fee that offsets the points value.
Solution: Pull the foreign currency earn rate from the relevant source URL for each card. Remind the user to factor in the foreign transaction fee (typically 1-3% on Israeli cards) when comparing net value of points earned abroad.

### User reports that their excluded-sector payment DID earn points

Cause: Merchant category code (MCC) assignment is done by the card network (Visa, Mastercard) and sometimes a merchant is coded differently than expected. For example, a private hospital may be coded as a clinic rather than a government hospital.
Solution: Clarify that the exclusion list applies to the MCC category, not the merchant's legal name. If the card issuer assigned a different MCC, points may accrue. The definitive answer is always the card issuer's customer service line or the printed terms.
