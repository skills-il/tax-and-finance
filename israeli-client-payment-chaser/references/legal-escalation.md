# Israeli Legal Framework for Debt Collection

## Demand Letter (Michtav Drisha / מכתב דרישה, also מכתב התראה לפני נקיטת הליכים)

### Legal Requirements
A proper demand letter under Israeli law should contain:
1. **Full identification** of both creditor and debtor (name, ID/company number, address)
2. **Description of the debt**: invoice numbers, dates, amounts, and the services/goods provided
3. **Total amount claimed**: principal amount plus any accrued interest and linkage (hatzmada)
4. **Payment deadline**: typically 14-30 days from the date of the letter
5. **Warning of legal proceedings**: explicit statement that failure to pay will result in filing a claim
6. **Payment instructions**: bank transfer details or other accepted payment methods

### Format Conventions
- Written in formal Hebrew
- Dated and signed by the creditor (or authorized representative)
- Sent via registered mail (doar rashum) for legal documentation
- Keep a copy of the letter and the postal receipt as proof

### Legal Standing
A demand letter is not legally required before filing a claim, but Israeli courts view it favorably. Sending a proper demand letter demonstrates good faith and strengthens the creditor's position. Courts may reduce legal costs awarded to a plaintiff who did not send a demand letter before filing.

## Small Claims Court (Beit Mishpat L'Tvi'ot Ktanot / בית משפט לתביעות קטנות)

### Jurisdiction and Threshold
- **Maximum claim amount:** 39,900 NIS (as of January 1, 2026; updated periodically, verify at the courts administration website)
- **Court location:** Determined by the debtor's place of residence or business address
- **Who can file:** private individuals only, which includes an osek murshe or osek patur. A company, partnership or amuta is barred: `חברה בע"מ, שותפות או עמותה לא יוכלו להגיש תביעה לבית משפט זה`. An incorporated claimant must use Magistrate Court regardless of the amount

### Filing Process
1. **Prepare documentation:** Gather original invoice, contract/purchase order, delivery confirmation, demand letters sent (with postal receipts), and communication history
2. **Complete the claim form:** Available at the court or online via the Small Claims service page at https://www.gov.il/he/service/filing_a_small_claim
3. **Pay filing fee:** 1% of the claim amount, minimum 50 NIS (2026)
4. **Submit the claim:** File at the Small Claims Court in the debtor's jurisdiction
5. **Serve the debtor:** The court sends the claim to the debtor, who has 30 days to respond
6. **Hearing:** Typically scheduled 30-60 days after filing. Both parties present their case

### Important Rules
- **Lawyer representation is barred by default, not absolutely.** The process does not require a lawyer (`ההליך בבית המשפט לתביעות קטנות אינו דורש ייצוג על-ידי עורך דין`), and representation is barred unless the court permits it on request under section 63 of the Courts Law. Special cases exist, including where a party is themselves a lawyer. Do not tell a user that lawyers are flatly forbidden or that they may never ask for representation
- **Evidence:** Bring all original documents. Printed communication logs (WhatsApp, email) are admissible
- **Judgment:** The judge issues a decision, typically on the same day or within a few days
- **Appeal:** Limited appeal rights, only to the District Court on points of law

### Multiple Claims to the Same Debtor
If multiple invoices to the same debtor exceed the Small Claims threshold when combined, you may:
- File separate claims for each invoice (if each is under the threshold)
- File a single claim in Magistrate Court (Beit Mishpat Shalom) for the full amount (representation is not required by law there, but is strongly advisable)

## Enforcement Office (Hotza'a LaPo'al / הוצאה לפועל)

A judgment does not collect itself. To enforce a debt the creditor opens a file with the Enforcement and Collection Authority (Rashut HaAchifa veHaGviya).

### Judgment-enforcement file (tik psak din)
- A request to enforce a monetary judgment can be filed only once **30 days have passed** from the date the judgment was given.
- Submit the judgment bearing the court stamp plus documents supporting the debt.
- **Opening fee:** roughly 1% of the debt at the date the file is opened, plus a protocol fee.
- Once open, the enforcement registrar can impose liens, garnish bank accounts and wages, and order asset seizure.
- Service page: https://www.gov.il/he/departments/law_enforcement_and_collection_system_authority

### Cheques and promissory notes file (tik shtarot ve'hamcha'ot), skips court
- A dishonored cheque (with the bank's Notice of Dishonor) or a signed promissory note can be filed **directly** at the Enforcement Office, with no prior court judgment.
- The debtor then has a short window to file an objection; if they object, the matter is referred to court.
- Required: the cheque printout or signed promissory note, plus identification of the debtor.
- Service page: https://www.gov.il/he/departments/law_enforcement_and_collection_system_authority

## Statutory Payment Deadline (Payment Ethics to Suppliers Law, 5777-2017)

The Payment Ethics to Suppliers Law (חוק מוסר תשלומים לספקים, תשע"ז-2017) sets when a payment to a supplier becomes legally late.

- **Default term when no payment term was agreed:** 45 days.
  - Private business: 45 days from the end of the month in which the invoice was submitted.
  - Government office / state body: 45 days from the date the invoice was submitted.
  - Local authority: 45 days from the end of the month in which the invoice was issued.
  - Engineering and construction contracts: longer caps, up to 85 days from invoice submission or 70 days from the end of the month of submission.
- An agreed contractual term overrides the default, subject to the caps the law imposes.
- Once the due date passes, the debt is legally late and linkage plus late-payment interest attach automatically.
- **What attaches to a late invoice, and when.** Section 4 of the law splits this into two components with different start dates: `תמורה הקבועה בחשבון שלא שולמה עד המועד לתשלום החשבון ... תשולם לספק בתוספת ריבית שקלית, ובחלוף 30 ימים מהמועד האמור - בתוספת דמי פיגורים`. So **ribit shkalit (shekel interest) runs from the payment due date**, and **dmei pigurim (late-payment fees) only begin 30 days AFTER that due date**. Do not describe dmei pigurim as running from the due date, and do not call the applicable figure "the Accountant General's rate": since Amendment 9 the rates are the statutory ribit shkalit / ribit tzmuda / dmei pigurim rates published quarterly. Verify the current quarter's rates before stating any percentage.

## Interest Calculation Rules

### Two distinct interest regimes
- **Pre-suit (supplier's claim):** Payment Ethics to Suppliers Law, 5777-2017. Ribit shkalit accrues from the due date; dmei pigurim only from 30 days after it. Used in demand letters.
- **On a judgment:** Adjudication of Interest and Linkage Law, set by the court. The agent does not compute this.
Do not quote the Bank of Israel base rate as the statutory late-payment rate; they are different figures.

### Adjudication of Interest and Linkage Law (חוק פסיקת ריבית והצמדה)
- **Accrual start date:** Interest begins from the invoice due date (not the invoice issue date)
- **Rate:** Bank of Israel base rate + statutory margin
- **Linkage (hatzmada):** In addition to interest, the debt amount is linked to the Consumer Price Index (CPI) from the due date
- **Compound interest:** Israeli law generally applies simple interest, not compound, unless otherwise agreed in the contract. Amendment 9 to the Interest and Linkage Law (effective January 1, 2025) further eliminated compound interest on enforcement debts and split late payment charges into "interest" (ribit) and "late payment fees" (dmei pigurim). Late payment fees now accrue quarterly starting 3 months after the due date, rather than compounding.
- **Verify current rate:** Check the Bank of Israel website (boi.org.il) for the current base rate at the time of calculation. Do NOT hard-code the rate here, it changes at each BoI rate decision, so any number written into this file goes stale within months.

### Contractual vs Statutory Interest
- If the contract specifies an interest rate, that rate applies (within legal limits)
- If no rate is specified, the statutory rate under the law applies
- Courts may reduce excessive contractual interest rates

## Registered Mail Documentation (Doar Rashum / דואר רשום)

### Sending Process
1. Prepare the demand letter in a sealed envelope
2. Go to any Israel Post (doar yisrael) branch
3. Request registered mail (doar rashum) service
4. Receive a tracking number and postal receipt
5. Keep the receipt. This is your proof of sending

### Legal Standing
- Registered mail with a retained receipt and tracking record is the standard proof of dispatch and is treated as strong evidence the letter was sent. Whether receipt is legally deemed depends on the applicable service rules, so do not promise the user that delivery is conclusively presumed
- Even if the debtor does not collect the letter, the postal receipt proves it was sent
- The tracking system shows delivery attempts and whether the letter was collected
- If returned uncollected, keep the returned envelope as additional evidence

### Alternative Delivery Methods
- **Process server (shaliah):** A licensed process server can deliver documents with a sworn affidavit of delivery
- **Email with confirmation:** Supplementary evidence but not a full substitute for registered mail
- **Fax with transmission report:** Accepted as supplementary evidence in some cases

## Shabbat and Holiday Schedule (for Reminder Blocking)

### Shabbat
- **Start:** Friday at sunset (varies by season and location; check hebcal.com or similar)
- **End:** Saturday after nightfall (approximately 40-72 minutes after sunset, varies by community)
- **Rule:** No reminders or collection communications during Shabbat

### Major Jewish Holidays (Chagim)
- Rosh Hashana (ראש השנה): 2 days, September/October
- Yom Kippur (יום כיפור): 1 day, September/October
- Sukkot (סוכות): first and last days, September/October
- Pesach (פסח): first and last days, March/April
- Shavuot (שבועות): 1 day, May/June

**Note:** Hebrew calendar dates shift relative to the Gregorian calendar each year. Always verify specific dates for the current year.
