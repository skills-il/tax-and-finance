# Israeli Legal Framework for Debt Collection

## Demand Letter (Michtav Drisha / מכתב דרישה, also מכתב התראה לפני נקיטת הליכים)

### Contents
A proper demand letter should contain:
1. **Full identification** of both creditor and debtor (name, ID/company number, address). Use the debtor's REGISTERED name
2. **Description of the debt**: invoice numbers, dates, amounts, and the services/goods provided
3. **Total amount claimed**: the principal, plus interest only as described under "Interest Calculation Rules" below
4. **Payment deadline**: a dated deadline at least 21 days after the letter date, so the debtor has 14 days after a registered letter arrives
5. **Warning of legal proceedings**: explicit statement that failure to pay will result in a claim or an enforcement file
6. **Payment instructions**: bank transfer details or other accepted payment methods

### Format Conventions
- Written in formal Hebrew
- Dated and signed by the creditor (or authorized representative)
- Sent by registered mail with delivery confirmation (דואר רשום עם אישור מסירה)
- Keep a copy of the letter, the postal receipt and the delivery confirmation

### Legal Standing
A demand letter is not legally required before filing a claim. It is still worth sending: it fixes the date the debtor plainly knew of the demand and evidences the collection effort. It is NOT the warning (hatra'a) the fixed-sum route requires, which is a separate document with prescribed wording (see "Fixed-sum claim" below).

## Small Claims Court (Beit Mishpat L'Tvi'ot Ktanot / בית משפט לתביעות קטנות)

### Jurisdiction and Threshold
- **Maximum claim amount:** 39,900 NIS (the 2026 indexed figure under sec. 60(a) of the Courts Law; it is updated periodically, verify at the courts administration website)
- **Who can file:** an individual only (`תביעה אזרחית שהגיש יחיד`), which includes an osek murshe or osek patur. A company, partnership or amuta is barred: `חברה בע"מ, שותפות או עמותה לא יוכלו להגיש תביעה לבית משפט זה`. An incorporated claimant must use Magistrate Court or the fixed-sum route at the Enforcement Office
- **Assigned claims are excluded.** Sec. 60(a) covers only claims that are not `לפי זכות שהומחתה או שהוסבה`, so a freelancer who sold or factored the invoice cannot sue on it here
- **Venue is the claimant's choice from a closed list** (reg. 2(a) of the Small Claims (Procedure) Regulations): the defendant's residence or place of business, or the place of the act or omission sued on. Paragraphs (2) to (4), which once covered where the undertaking was made or was to be performed, are marked (נמחקה) and must not be relied on. The rule applies `על אף האמור בכל הסכם שבין בעלי הדין`, so a contractual venue clause does not bind. Reg. 2(b) limits a claim over online publication or online trade to the defendant's or the plaintiff's residence or business

### Filing Process
1. **Prepare documentation:** original invoice, contract or purchase order, delivery confirmation, demand letters sent (with postal receipts and delivery confirmations), and communication history
2. **Complete the claim form:** online or at the court, via the Small Claims service page at https://www.gov.il/he/service/filing_a_small_claim
3. **Pay the filing fee:** 1% of the claim, never less than 50 NIS, rounded up to a multiple of 5 (reg. 6(a))
4. **Submit the claim** at a court in any of the venues above
5. **The defendant is summoned** to file a defence within 30 days of service (reg. 4(b)). A defendant who files no defence in time may have judgment given on the claim alone (reg. 10). A judgment given in the debtor's absence can also change enforcement timing (see "Judgment file" below)
6. **Hearing:** both parties present their case and their documents

### Important Rules
- **Representation by a lawyer is the exception.** It is permitted only with the court's leave and for special reasons recorded (sec. 63(a) of the Courts Law: `רק ברשות בית המשפט ומטעמים מיוחדים שיירשמו`). Do not tell a user that lawyers are flatly forbidden, or that one is required
- **Evidence:** Bring all original documents. Printed communication logs (WhatsApp, email) are commonly submitted
- **Appeal is by leave only.** A judgment can be appealed to the District Court only with the leave of a District Court judge (sec. 64). The request for leave is due within 30 days of the judgment for proceedings opened from 11.4.2025, and 15 days for proceedings opened earlier (reg. 16(a))

### Several Invoices to the Same Debtor
Do not split one engagement into several small claims just to stay under the cap; the court may transfer a claim it considers unsuitable to Magistrate Court (sec. 60(b)). Where the total exceeds the threshold, consider the fixed-sum route (up to 75,000 NIS) or a single Magistrate Court claim, where representation is not required by law but is strongly advisable.

## Enforcement Office (Hotza'a LaPo'al / הוצאה לפועל)

A judgment does not collect itself. The creditor opens a file with the Enforcement and Collection Authority (Rashut HaAchifa veHaGviya). There are three kinds of file, and two of them need no judgment.

### Judgment file (tik psak din)
- **When you may file (Execution Law sec. 6(b)).** Only after the date for performance set in the judgment; where none is set, after 30 days from the judgment, and where the judgment was given in the debtor's absence and the creditor had to serve it, 30 days from service. Under sec. 6(b1) the registrar may allow earlier filing, on a request supported by affidavit, where not opening a file could frustrate enforcement.
- **Do not sit on it.** A judgment or a note filed more than a year after it could first have been filed carries only ribit shkalit for the gap (sec. 6(b2)).
- **The azhara.** Once the file is opened the debtor is served a warning and has 20 days from service to pay or apply for an instalment order (sec. 7(a)). Nothing coercive happens before service and the end of that period, unless the registrar finds the circumstances justify acting earlier (sec. 7(e)).
- **Opening fee:** 1% of the value at the time of filing, subject to an indexed minimum, plus a protocol fee (Execution (Fees, Remuneration and Expenses) Regulations, schedule item (a)(1) and reg. 2a). Confirm the current minimum on the Authority's site.

### Fixed-sum claim (tvi'a al skhum katzuv, sec. 81a1), skips court
- **What qualifies:** a claim for a fixed sum arising from an express contract or undertaking with written evidence (`תביעה על סכום כסף קצוב הבאה מכוח חוזה או התחייבות מפורשים, שיש עליה ראיה בכתב`), up to 75,000 NIS on the day of filing (`ובלבד שסכום התביעה אינו עולה על 75,000 שקלים חדשים ביום הגשתה`). Open to a company claimant as well as an individual.
- **The evidence must be signed by the debtor.** Reg. 109d1(a) of the Execution Regulations defines the supporting document as `חוזה, הסכם או כל ראיה בכתב חתומה בידי הנתבע, בחתימת ידו או בחתימה אלקטרונית מאושרת`. A quote or order the client signed qualifies; an email or WhatsApp acceptance, a PO generated by the client's system, or the freelancer's own quote signed only by the freelancer usually does not. Reg. 109d1(b)-(c) has special rules for a distance sale, and the office may refuse a request without the documents (reg. 109d1(d)).
- **The warning first, in the prescribed form.** The warning must prominently include the wording of Form 19א in the First Schedule to the Regulations, and set out the amount, the main facts of the cause of action including the contract details, the date it arose, and the supporting documents (reg. 109a(a): `תכלול באופן בולט לעין את האמור בנוסח ההתראה שבטופס 19א`). Use the Authority's form rather than improvising one. It is served by registered mail with delivery confirmation (sec. 81a1(b1)); if that fails and the creditor has a lawyer, the lawyer may serve it personally. A debtor who refuses the letter or refuses to sign the confirmation is treated as served (sec. 81a1(b2)). An uncollected letter is neither delivered nor refused, so it is not service.
- **Debtor details:** no proceedings, including service of the azhara, without confirmation of the debtor's identifying details (reg. 109ya), and against an individual the details must match the Population Registry (sec. 6(d)(2)).
- **Timing:** file only after 30 days from service of the warning (sec. 81a1(c)(1)). After a year, a fresh warning is needed (sec. 81a1(c)(2)). The request attaches the statement of claim, the supporting documents, a copy of the warning, the dispatch and delivery confirmations, and the debtor's reply if any.
- **Objection:** the debtor has 30 days from service of the azhara to object, with an affidavit; an objection stays enforcement and sends the claim and the objection to court (sec. 81a1(d)). The registrar also transfers to court a claim that does not meet the conditions (sec. 81a1(f)).
- **Fee:** the same as a judgment file, 1% subject to the indexed minimum, plus the protocol fee. If the matter later goes to court on an objection the court accepts, what was paid at the Enforcement Office is credited against the court fee (Court Fees Regulations reg. 4).
- **No early action.** Unlike a judgment file, nothing can be enforced before the azhara is served and its period has passed (sec. 81a1(e)(2)), so this route does not help against a debtor who is emptying the company.
- **Not for a disputed debt.** An objection turns it into a court case anyway; the cost is delay rather than a lost fee.
- **Suing in court instead has a price.** A note or a fixed-sum claim that could have been filed at the Enforcement Office and is filed in court instead normally earns no award of lawyer's fees, unless a temporary remedy was granted or special circumstances justified court (sec. 81a2).

### Cheques and promissory notes file (tik shtarot ve'hamcha'ot), skips court
- A dishonoured cheque (with the bank's notice of dishonour) or a signed promissory note can be filed **directly** at the Enforcement Office, with no prior court judgment.
- The debtor may object within 30 days of service of the azhara (sec. 81a(c1)); if they object, the matter goes to court, and the fee paid is credited as above.
- **Opening fee:** 1.25% of the value at the time of filing, subject to an indexed minimum, plus the protocol fee (schedule item (a)(6)).
- **Cash Reduction Law trap:** a cheque that a bank may not pay under sec. 5 of the Cash Reduction Law (for example, one where the payee's name is not written, `שם הנפרע אינו נקוב בשיק`) cannot be filed as a note without first meeting the fixed-sum warning rules (sec. 81a(g)). When taking post-dated cheques, make sure each names the creditor as payee and is not endorsed onward.

### What the registrar can do once the file is live
- **Third-party attachment (sec. 44).** An order served on a bank, or on a client of the debtor, catches the debtor's assets the third party holds on service or receives within three months after; the third party must report within 10 days. This is the workhorse of a live file.
- **Restrictions (sec. 66a), a closed list:** barring an Israeli passport or travel document; an exit ban; designation as a lakoach mugbal meyuchad under the Cheques Without Cover Law, 5741-1981; barring use of a payment card; and barring the debtor from founding a corporation or being a baal inyan in one. Paragraph (6) is marked deleted. There is **no** driving-licence restriction and **no** power to stop the debtor opening bank accounts. Do not confuse these with a declaration of חייב מוגבל באמצעים (sec. 69d), a separate status for a debtor found of limited means: it comes with an instalment order and its own set of restrictions on the debtor.
- **When restrictions are available (sec. 66b).** Not on demand. Either the debtor was brought before the registrar and shown able to pay yet evading, with no reasonable explanation, on a judgment debt above 500 NIS (sec. 66b(a)(1)); or the debtor is deemed an evader under the Act and six months have passed from full service of the azhara with debts above 2,500 NIS, or a year with debts above 500 NIS (sec. 66b(a)(3)). Restrictions also need the azhara to have been served by full service or the debtor to have appeared (sec. 7(c)), which is why service matters.
- **Ability investigation (chakirat yecholet).** Where the debtor claims they cannot pay, ask the registrar to examine their means. It tests the claim and produces the asset information the other steps depend on.
- **Fees come back.** The creditor pays fees and expenses up front (sec. 9(a)), but they are charged to the debtor and treated as part of the debt (sec. 9(b)), with ribit shkalit from the day they were paid (sec. 9(d)).
- **Consolidation (ichud tikim).** A debtor may apply to consolidate all files against them (sec. 74b). Proceedings continue until a consolidation order is made (sec. 74c(a)); after that the creditor is paid through the consolidated arrangement.

## Statutory Payment Deadline (Payment Ethics to Suppliers Law, 5777-2017)

The Payment Ethics to Suppliers Law (חוק מוסר תשלומים לספקים, תשע"ז-2017) sets when a payment to a supplier becomes legally late. The clock runs from when the invoice was SUBMITTED (הומצא) to the purchaser.

- **Who is covered:** a state body, a local authority, a budgeted or statutory body (sec. 3(e)(1)), and a "business", defined by VAT status: `מוסד כספי, עוסק מורשה או עוסק פטור כהגדרתם בחוק מס ערך מוסף` (sec. 2, with listed exceptions). Transactions between health institutions and suppliers for National Health Insurance services are excluded (sec. 12(c)), and the local-authority term in sec. 3(f) does not apply to a local authority in recovery (sec. 12(d)). A consumer is not covered.
- **Default term when no payment term was agreed:**
  - Private business (sec. 3(z)): 45 days from the end of the month in which the invoice was submitted.
  - Budgeted or statutory body (sec. 3(e)(1)): 45 days from the end of the month of submission; a different date needs the body's director-general's approval and must pass the same special-nature or not-exceptionally-unfair test. Sec. 10(a) provides that the state-body terms in sec. 3(a) and (b) apply to these bodies a year after commencement unless otherwise determined, so confirm which term governs from the contract or the body's payment terms.
  - State body (sec. 3(a)): two alternatives, the contract decides which applies: 45 days from submission, or 30 days from the end of the month of submission.
  - Local authority (sec. 3(f)(1)): 45 days from the end of the month in which the invoice was submitted; 80 days for engineering and construction work. Where the deal is externally funded and the authority gave written notice of that at contracting, it may defer the funded share until 10 business days after the funding arrives, and in any case no later than 150 days from submission (sec. 3(f)(2)).
  - State engineering and construction contracts (sec. 3(b)): up to 85 days from submission or 70 days from the end of the month of submission.
- **An agreed term:** against a private business, a different date must be set expressly in the contract, and it may be set only if the special nature of the engagement requires it or it is not an exceptionally unfair date (`אלא אם כן הדבר נדרש בשל אופייה המיוחד של ההתקשרות או שהוא אינו מועד בלתי הוגן באופן חריג`). There is no fixed cap in days for a private business. The law cannot be contracted out of except in the supplier's favour (sec. 7: `אין להתנות על הוראות חוק זה אלא לטובת הספק`).
- **Submission (sec. 3(i)):** personal delivery, registered mail with delivery confirmation, an electronic message with an electronic delivery confirmation, fax with confirmation, the purchaser's own system, or another agreed method. Keep the proof.
- **A returned invoice (sec. 3(h)):** a purchaser who finds a material detail missing returns the invoice listing the defects, and it is treated as not submitted (`ויראו זאת כאילו החשבון לא הומצא למזמין`). That applies to a return within the review period, 23 business days from submission for most clients and 60 days for construction. A return after that period does not reset the clock: the client pays within 10 business days of the original due date, and the time until the corrected invoice arrives is not counted (sec. 3(h)(2)). A business or a 3(e) body may agree different rules, subject to the same fairness test (sec. 3(h)(3)).
- **What attaches to a late invoice, and when (sec. 4(a)):** `תמורה הקבועה בחשבון שלא שולמה עד המועד לתשלום החשבון ... תשולם לספק בתוספת ריבית שקלית, ובחלוף 30 ימים מהמועד האמור - בתוספת דמי פיגורים`. So **ribit shkalit runs from the payment due date** and **dmei pigurim only from 30 days after it**. The section attaches no linkage.
- **The condition for a business purchaser (sec. 4(b)):** `הוראות סעיף קטן (א) יחולו על התקשרות בין מזמין שהוא גוף כאמור בסעיף 3(ה)(1) או עסק כאמור בסעיף 3(ז) ובין ספק, רק בהתקשרות שבה היתה לאותו גוף או עסק, לפי העניין, עדיפות בעיצוב תנאי החוזה`. Against a private business or a 3(e)(1) body, sec. 4 interest applies only where that client had the advantage in shaping the contract, which is a question of bargaining power rather than of who drafted the document. A client's own purchase order or standard supplier terms points toward it; a large client that accepted a freelancer's quote may still have had it. When unclear, use the neutral formula. Sec. 4(c) keeps every other remedy open, including interest a court awards under the Adjudication of Interest and Linkage Law.

## Interest Calculation Rules

### Two distinct interest regimes
- **Pre-suit (supplier's claim):** Payment Ethics to Suppliers Law, sec. 4, subject to sec. 4(b). Ribit shkalit accrues from the due date; dmei pigurim only from 30 days after it.
- **Awarded by a court:** Adjudication of Interest and Linkage Law, set by the court. The agent does not compute this.
Do not quote the Bank of Israel monetary-policy rate as either.

### Adjudication of Interest and Linkage Law (חוק פסיקת ריבית והצמדה)
- **Start date:** by default the period runs from the day the claim was filed (sec. 2(b): `מיום הגשת התביעה ועד מועד הפירעון`). The court may set an earlier start, but not before the cause of action arose. So a demand letter may ask for interest from the due date, but the agent must not tell the user they will get it from that date.
- **What the court awards:** ribit shkalit, or by discretion linkage differentials, linked interest with linkage, or a different rate, the last three only for reasons that justify it (sec. 2(a)). An award of "interest" that does not name a type is ribit shkalit only (sec. 2(d)). Linkage is therefore not automatic.
- **Rates:** since Amendment 9 (in force 1 January 2025 under the postponement order) the statute works through three separately published annual rates, `ריבית שקלית`, `ריבית צמודה` and `דמי פיגורים`, set by regulation and published quarterly (sec. 5c). There is no "Bank of Israel rate plus a margin" formula.
- **Machine-readable source:** the `data.gov.il` dataset "ריביות החשב הכללי" (<https://data.gov.il/api/3/action/datastore_search?resource_id=d1cdadd7-f6b6-40a2-aab9-73230d5fe294>), whose rows carry `interestname`, an `ineffecfrom` effective date and an `annual` rate. Read the row whose effective date covers the period. Do NOT read the rate off a law-firm calculator page; several publish a stale quarter.
- **The rate re-rolls every quarter**, so no figure is written into this file. If it cannot be confirmed, use the safe formulation in a letter and let the court or the registrar compute it: `הקרן בתוספת ריבית והצמדה כדין ממועד הפירעון`.
- **Compounding:** daily ribit is added up and capitalised into the principal once every 12 months (sec. 4a(b)), so interest does compound annually. Dmei pigurim are never capitalised (sec. 5a(d)).
- **Dmei pigurim under this law:** added every three months, starting three months after the due date (sec. 5a(a): `דמי פיגורים יתווספו מדי שלושה חודשים החל מתום שלושה חודשים ממועד הפירעון`). This is the court and enforcement regime. It is a different clock from the suppliers law's 30 days, so do not merge the two.

### Contractual vs Statutory Interest
- Where the contract sets interest for late payment, the court does not add interest under this law (sec. 6(a)), and where it sets late-payment charges, no dmei pigurim are added under it (sec. 6(c))
- Where the contract is silent, the statutory regime applies as described above

## Registered Mail Documentation (Doar Rashum / דואר רשום)

### Sending Process
1. Prepare the demand letter in a sealed envelope
2. Go to any Israel Post (doar yisrael) branch
3. Request registered mail with delivery confirmation (doar rashum im ishur mesira)
4. Receive a tracking number and postal receipt
5. Keep the receipt. This is your proof of sending

### Legal Standing
- Registered mail with a retained receipt and tracking record is the standard proof of dispatch and is treated as strong evidence the letter was sent. Whether receipt is legally deemed depends on the applicable service rules, so do not promise the user that delivery is conclusively presumed
- Even if the debtor does not collect the letter, the postal receipt proves it was sent
- The tracking system shows delivery attempts and whether the letter was collected
- If returned uncollected, keep the returned envelope as additional evidence

### Alternative Delivery Methods
- **Process server (shaliah):** A licensed process server can deliver documents with a sworn affidavit of delivery
- **Email with confirmation:** Supplementary evidence but not a full substitute for registered mail
- **Fax with transmission report:** Accepted as supplementary evidence in some cases

## Major Jewish Holidays (Chagim)
- Rosh Hashana (ראש השנה): 2 days, September/October
- Yom Kippur (יום כיפור): 1 day, September/October
- Sukkot (סוכות): first and last days, September/October
- Pesach (פסח): first and last days, March/April
- Shavuot (שבועות): 1 day, May/June

Hebrew calendar dates shift relative to the Gregorian calendar each year, so verify the current year's dates. The Shabbat and chag boundaries are set by the rule below, not by sunset.

## Shabbat and chag scheduling: the deterministic rule

Reminder timing is the one part of this skill an agent has to compute rather than look up, so the rule is stated once, here, in full.

**Never send on:** Friday from candle-lighting until Saturday nightfall; a chag, on the same sunset-to-nightfall boundaries; and the afternoon of erev chag and erev Shabbat, which is commercially dead even though it is not halachically prohibited.

**The boundaries, precisely:**

- Shabbat begins at **candle-lighting**, conventionally 18 to 40 minutes before sunset depending on the city (Jerusalem uses 40, most of the country 18 to 20). Use candle-lighting, not sunset, as the cut-off.
- Shabbat ends at **nightfall (tzeit hakochavim)**, roughly 35 to 45 minutes after sunset by the common Israeli conventions. Where a source offers a range, take the LATER value; sending three minutes early is the error that gets noticed.
- Sunset differs by roughly 20 minutes across Israel, so resolve times for the RECIPIENT's city, not yours, and not a national average.
- If the debtor is abroad, use their local Shabbat times, and note that chagim outside Israel run an extra day.

**Also treat as blocked or degraded:**

- **Chol hamoed** (Pesach and Sukkot): a partial working period. Not blocked, but do not expect a reply, and never let a deadline you set expire inside it.
- **Tisha B'Av** and the **Yom HaZikaron / Yom HaAtzmaut** pair: commercially dead and, in the case of Yom HaZikaron, actively inappropriate for a debt demand.
- **Purim** is a working day in most of the country and a holiday in Jerusalem (Shushan Purim), so it depends on the recipient's city.

**Non-Jewish recipients:** the Saturday block is not automatically right and the Friday block may be, for different reasons. Where the client's community is known, use their rest day; where it is not, weekday business hours are the safe default.

**When the calendar source is unavailable** (no `hebcal` MCP, no network): do not silently skip the check and send anyway. Fall back to sending only Sunday to Thursday, 09:00 to 16:00 recipient-local, which is inside every boundary above in every week of the year, and tell the user the reminder was conservatively scheduled.

**Resolve times from** <https://www.hebcal.com>, which publishes candle-lighting and havdalah per city, or the `hebcal` MCP.
