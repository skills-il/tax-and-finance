## Mortgage (Mashkanta) Guidelines
### BOI Regulations
- Max LTV: 75% (first home), 70% (home replacement), 50% (investment)
- Mix rule: at least 1/3 of the total mortgage must be in a FIXED-interest track. The fixed third does NOT have to be unlinked; a CPI-linked fixed track satisfies it
- Up to 2/3 of the total mortgage may be variable/prime
- Maximum term: 30 years on any track
- The caps come from the Bank of Israel's Proper Conduct of Banking Business directives on housing loans

### Indicative Rate Ranges (2026)
Market levels for orientation only. These are not published or regulated figures and no authoritative source publishes them; price the actual tracks with the bank.
| Track | Type | Range |
|-------|------|-------|
| Prime-linked | Variable | Prime +/- 0.5% |
| Fixed unlinked | Fixed | 4.5%-6.5% |
| CPI-linked fixed | Fixed + CPI | 3.0%-5.0% + CPI |
| CPI-linked variable | Resets 5yr | 2.5%-4.5% + CPI |

### Payment-to-Income (PTI)
- BOI regulatory cap: monthly repayment may not exceed 50% of disposable income
- 50% is the regulatory ceiling, not a target. Individual banks underwrite more strictly below it; ask the bank what ratio it will actually approve

## Net-Pay Deductions (Employee, 2026)
These feed the affordability math (net income drives PTI and budget headroom). Rates are the salaried-employee shares, verified against Bituach Leumi (btl.gov.il).
- Bituach Leumi + health-tax (employee): the rate varies by age and pension status. The BTL rate table publishes a row per category; the figures below are the COMBINED employee rate (BL plus health-tax) per row, reduced band / full band:

| Employee category | Reduced band | Full band |
|---|---|---|
| Resident aged 18 to retirement age (default) | 4.27% (1.04% BL + 3.23% health) | 12.17% (7% BL + 5.17% health) |
| Under 18 | 0% | 0% |
| Receiving old-age pension | 0% | 0% |
| Receiving disability pension, with annual approval | 3.23% (health only) | 5.17% (health only) |
| Aged 67 to 70, not receiving old-age pension | 3.93% | 10.03% |
| Woman between retirement age and male retirement age, not receiving a pension | 3.95% | 10.24% |
| Controlling shareholder in a close company | 4.25% | 11.96% |
| First became resident after age 62, below retirement age | 3.6% | 7.45% |

  The percentages carry 2025 effective dates and did not change for 2026; only the thresholds did. The BL/health split within a combined figure is derived by subtracting the health-tax leg.
- Reduced-collection step: 7,703 NIS/month (from 1 Jan 2026); both BL and health-tax bands split here. Do not describe this step as a fixed fraction of the average wage: it reconciles with neither the section 1 (13,566) nor the section 2 (13,769) average, and the Bituach Leumi page states it only as an absolute amount.
- Maximum income subject to insurance (ceiling): 51,910 NIS/month (from 1 Jan 2026).
- Income tax: marginal 10%-50% brackets, widened retroactively from 1 Jan 2026 (the 20% step runs to 228,000 NIS/year and the 31% step to 301,200 NIS/year).
- Surtax (mas yesef): the 50% top row above 721,560 NIS/year (60,130/month) is the 47% bracket plus a 3% surtax. Since 2025 a further 2% applies to high income that is not from employment or a business, taking the top rate there to 52%.
- Tax credit point (nekudat zikui): 242 NIS/month (2026); resident base 2.25, women +0.5 (2.75).
- Pension contribution: employee 6% (statutory minimum). Employer adds 6.5% tagmulim plus 6% severance, for 18.5% total. The 6.5% figure is the employer leg and must NOT be deducted from the employee.

## Arnona (Municipal Property Tax)
Arnona is set by each municipality and updated annually via the tzav arnona (arnona order). Per-city tables go stale within a year or two, so do not hardcode them here. To get an accurate figure:
- Fetch the relevant municipality's current-year tzav arnona (2026) from its website.
- Note the rate is per square meter, varies by zone and property classification, and is billed bi-monthly in most cities.
- For housing indices and inflation context, use the israeli-cbs MCP (cbs.gov.il).
