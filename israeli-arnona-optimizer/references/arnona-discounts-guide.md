# Arnona Discounts Guide

## Legal Framework

Arnona discounts in Israel are governed by two layers of regulation:

1. **National regulations**: the Arrangements in the State Economy Regulations (Arnona Discount), 5753-1993. Read the consolidated text at <https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_הסדרים_במשק_המדינה_(הנחה_מארנונה)> before quoting any figure. Most of its paragraphs are CEILINGS a council may set within, not floors every municipality must offer. Chapter Hey2 (Regs. 14e, 14e1) and Regs. 3c and 3c1 are the exception and are true entitlements. The Senior Citizens Law 5750-1989 s.9 supplies a separate statutory senior entitlement.
2. **Municipal bylaws**: each municipality decides where within each ceiling it sits, and may add categories the national regulation does not contain at all (student and large-family discounts are the two common examples).

**Every percentage in this guide is traceable to a numbered paragraph.** Where the regulation is silent, this guide says so rather than supplying a plausible figure. Do not fill such a gap.

## Discount Categories

### 1. Oleh Chadash (New Immigrant)

**Discount**: Up to 90% on up to 100 sqm, for 12 months that the oleh may choose within the first 24 months from the date registered as an oleh (teudat oleh / population-registry date)

**Eligibility**:
- Valid teudat oleh (immigrant certificate) from the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration
- The property must be the primary residence
- Applies to the first 100 sqm only (area above 100 sqm charged at full rate)
- An oleh who lived in another municipality first must bring confirmation from it that they did not receive the oleh discount there

**Required documents**:
- Teudat oleh (original + copy)
- Teudat zehut (ID card)
- Lease agreement or property deed (tabu extract)
- Arnona account details

**Important notes**:
- The 12 discounted months can be chosen within the first 24 months from the population-registry oleh registration, or from the date of an oleh-citizen certificate (they need not be the first 12 consecutive months)
- When the oleh period ends, check the other rows (low income, senior, and so on)

### 2. Active-Duty Soldier (Chayyal Sadir)

**Discount**: 100%, an entitlement under Reg. 14e(1), not a municipal choice. Capped at 70 sqm of the flat, rising to 90 sqm where the number of the holder's family members living with them exceeds four (Reg. 14f).

**Eligibility**:
- Serving conscript service in the IDF, and, for the conscript only, four months after discharge (Reg. 14e(1)(a))
- The same 100% covers a national-service volunteer, a full-track civilian-service member, a full-track guarding-civilian-service member averaging 40 weekly hours over 12 months, and a civilian-security service member
- A parent the soldier supported before service, who has no livelihood and cannot obtain one, is separately entitled to 100% under Reg. 14e(1)(b), provided the soldier is exempt under 14e(1)(a)
- The soldier must be the holder of the property

**Required documents**:
- IDF service confirmation letter (ishur sherut)
- Teudat zehut
- Lease agreement

**Important notes**:
- The entitlement belongs to the soldier as HOLDER. A soldier living in a flat held by their parents is not the holder there; the parents' own rows apply to that flat
- Career (keva) service is not conscript service and is not covered by Reg. 14e(1)

### 3. Senior Citizen (Ezrach Vatik)

The senior discount is structured by pension/income status, NOT by age band (65-69 vs 70+):

**25% (discretionary)**:
- Receives an old-age, survivors, or work-injury pension from Bituach Leumi. No income test.

**30% (statutory entitlement, Senior Citizens Law 5750-1989 s.9(b))**:
- The senior's total income from every source must not exceed the average wage as last published before the application. Where more than one senior lives in the flat, the combined income of everyone living there must not exceed 150% of the average wage (s.9(c)(4)).
- Given for one flat only, and to one senior only, even where several qualify (s.9(c)(1) and (2)).
- Once granted it renews automatically. A senior on the 30% rate who has not yet turned 70 keeps automatic renewal for three calendar years after the year the discount was granted, or until turning 70, whichever comes first, and must then re-apply (s.9(d)).

**100% (statutory entitlement, s.9(b))**:
- Where the senior receives a benefit under the Income Support Law 5741-1980. Reg. 2(a)(1)(b) is the parallel discretionary ceiling at the same figure.
- **Low-pension seniors (s.13A)**: every benefit given to an income-support recipient, including this 100%, also goes to a senior whose pension income is at most 24.3% of the average wage (38.3% together with a spouse) and who would receive income support but for that pension. Applies only to seniors first insured from the comprehensive-pension extension order onward; Bituach Leumi checks eligibility.
- **Reporting duty (s.9(e))**: a senior on automatic renewal who stops meeting a condition must report it to the municipality in writing within two months; a municipality that learns of it otherwise must give the senior two months to respond before cancelling.

**Eligibility (all tiers)**:
- Israeli resident at retirement age
- Property is primary residence
- Applies to first 100 sqm

**Required documents**:
- Teudat zehut (proving age)
- Income declarations (tax assessments, pension statements, Bituach Leumi statements)
- For couples: spouse's income documentation as well
- Arnona account details

**Important notes**:
- The s.9 discount applies even where only one of the spouses living in the flat is a senior (s.9(c)(3))
- Only one discount applies per flat (Reg. 17(a)); a senior who also fits another row gets the higher one, never both
- **Old-age pension for the disabled (קצבת זיקנה לנכה)**: 100% on 100 sqm where total income from every source is within the average wage, or within 150% of it for everyone living in the flat (Kol Zchut, citing the Senior Citizens (Benefits for a Senior Receiving an Old-Age Pension for the Disabled) Regulations)

### 4. Disabled Person (Adam Im Mugbalut)

**Medical disability 90% or more (Reg. 2(a)(3))**:
- **Ceiling**: up to 40%. Proven under any law, and it also covers a determination made before old-age pension began. The regulation states no area cap for this row.

**Earning incapacity 75% or more, with a full monthly benefit (Reg. 2(a)(2))**:
- **Ceiling**: up to 80%, under section 127lamed-vav of the National Insurance Law, including a permanent determination made before old-age pension began. No area cap stated.

**Blind person's certificate (Reg. 2(a)(5))**:
- **Ceiling**: up to 90% for a holder of a certificate under the Welfare Services Law 5718-1958. No area cap stated. This is the single largest ceiling in Regulation 2 and is routinely missed.

**Long-term nursing benefit, gimlat siud (Reg. 2(a)(7)(c))**:
- **Ceiling**: up to 70% for a recipient under Chapter Vav of the National Insurance Law. No area cap stated.

**Parent of a child entitled to the disabled-child benefit (Reg. 2(a)(11))**:
- **Ceiling**: up to 33% on 100 sqm, where a child of the holder, INCLUDING A FOSTER CHILD, is entitled to a benefit under the National Insurance (Disabled Child) Regulations 5770-2010, or is over 18 and receives a disability benefit that was preceded by the disabled-child benefit.
- **Per child, up to 90% combined**: the Arrangements Law 5753-1992 s.12(g) overrides the regulation. The discount is computed for EACH child for whom a disability benefit is paid, including a child over 18 who never received the disabled-child benefit, and the total may not exceed 90%. Two entitled children: up to 66%.

**Disabled veterans and equivalent (Reg. 14e(2)) are an ENTITLEMENT of two thirds**, not a ceiling: a disabled person under the Invalids (Pensions and Rehabilitation) Law, the Nazi-war Invalids Law, the Police (Disabled and Fallen) Law or the Prison Service (Disabled and Fallen) Law, a bereaved family member under the Families of Soldiers Who Fell Law, and a hostile-action casualty under the 5730-1970 Law. Capped at 70 sqm, 90 sqm where more than four family members live with the holder.

Note: the regulation defines no intermediate disability band between these two. A determination that falls below both thresholds carries no national entitlement, though a municipality may still grant something under its own bylaw.

**Eligibility**:
- Disability determination from the National Insurance Institute (Bituach Leumi) or the Ministry of Defense (for security-related disability)
- Property is primary residence
- The area cap is per row: none stated for 2(2), 2(3), 2(5) and 2(7)(c); 100 sqm for 2(11); 70 sqm (90 for a large household) for Reg. 14e(2)

**Required documents**:
- Disability determination letter from Bituach Leumi (showing percentage)
- Teudat zehut
- Arnona account details

### 5. Low Income (Hachnasa Nemukha)

The thresholds are per household by headcount, not per person. The municipality chooses where within each ceiling it sits.

**What "income" excludes** (Reg. 2(a)(8)(b)): benefits under Chapters Daled and Yod-Alef of the National Insurance Law and the disabled-child benefit; half the monthly income of a son or daughter living with the holder, and for ONE son or daughter the first slice up to the minimum wage is disregarded entirely; rent received for the holder's flat up to the rent they pay for another flat they rent to live in; and special and additional reserve-service payments under the Reserve Service Law 5768-2008. "Average monthly income" for an employee is the average over the twelve months preceding the fiscal year; for a self-employed person it is derived from the last annual tax assessment, divided by 12 and index-adjusted.

**Required documents**:
- Pay slips (tlushei maskoret) for the twelve months of the tested year, or the annual tax assessment for the self-employed
- Bituach Leumi benefit statements
- The same for every person living in the flat whose income counts
- Bank statements (some municipalities)

**Important notes**:
- Income is the gross income of the holder AND everyone living with them, from every source the regulation does not exclude; maintenance (mezonot) received counts
- The thresholds are fixed by the regulation; the council chooses only the percentage within each band's ceiling, and may set sub-tests under Reg. 2a
- The reservist-pay exclusion (Reg. 2(a)(8)(c)) was a temporary order for 2024 and 2025 income and has lapsed
- Renewal required annually

### 6. Student (Student)

**Discount**: none nationally. The Arnona Discount Regulations contain NO student paragraph. Any student discount is purely a municipal bylaw creation, so there is no national rate to quote and none should be assumed. Read the municipality's own discount table.

**Eligibility**: whatever the municipality's own bylaw says. There is no national criterion to describe. A student's national route is the Reg. 2(a)(8) income test, applied to everyone living in the flat.

### 7. Single Parent (Horeh Yachid)

**Discount**: a national ceiling of up to 20% exists at Reg. 2(a)(10). The council chooses where within that ceiling it sits, and may sit at zero, but the paragraph is national and should be cited when applying. The regulation states no area cap for this row.

**Eligibility (Reg. 2(a)(10))**:
- A single parent as defined in the Single-Parent Families Law 5752-1992 (the definition, not marital status, decides; a divorced parent whose children live with the other parent should be checked against it); OR
- A single parent of a child living with them who is serving conscript service under the Security Service Law, or is a national-service volunteer as defined in Reg. 14d, for as long as they serve and provided their age does not exceed 21
- Under Reg. 2a the council may take the holder's material position into account and set sub-tests for this and the other Reg. 2(1) to (10) paragraphs

**Required documents**:
- Single parent recognition (from Bituach Leumi or court order)
- Children's birth certificates
- Income documentation
- Arnona account details

### 8. Large Family (Mishpacha Brukat Yeladim)

**Discount**: none nationally. The Arnona Discount Regulations contain NO large-family paragraph. Do not quote a national rate. The national route for a large household is the Reg. 2(a)(8) income test in section 5 above, whose thresholds rise with each additional person, so a large household qualifies at a higher income than a small one. A municipality may add its own category on top.

**Eligibility**: whatever the municipality's own bylaw says, where one exists. There is no national criterion to describe.

**Required documents**:
- Children's birth certificates or teudat zehut entries
- Income documentation
- Arnona account details

### 9. Bereaved Family (Mishpacha Shkula)

**Discount**: two thirds, an ENTITLEMENT under Reg. 14e(2)(d), capped at 70 sqm (90 sqm where more than four family members live with the holder). The same two-thirds entitlement covers disabled veterans, Nazi-war disabled, police and prison-service disabled, and hostile-action casualties under Reg. 14e(2)(a) to (g). A holder of non-residential premises may claim the same rate under Reg. 14z if they owed no income-tax advance that year and the assessing officer certified it

**Eligibility**:
- Family of a fallen serviceperson, a hostile-action casualty, or a police or prison-service casualty, as recognised under the law each Reg. 14e(2) paragraph names
- Property is primary residence

**Required documents**:
- Recognition letter from the body that pays the benefit (Ministry of Defense, Bituach Leumi for hostile-action casualties, the Police or the Prison Service)
- Teudat zehut
- Arnona account details

**Important notes**:
- This is a permanent benefit, not time-limited
- The area cap is 70 sqm, or 90 sqm where more than four family members live with the holder (Reg. 14f), not 100 sqm

### 10. Persecution and Prisoner-of-Zion Pensions (Reg. 2(a)(4))

**Discount**: a ceiling of up to 66% on 70 sqm, rising to 90 sqm where the number of family members living with the entitled person exceeds four. Note the AREA cap: this row is 70/90 sqm, not 100.

**Eligibility (the paragraph is a closed list of six benefits)**:
- A Prisoner of Zion benefit, or a benefit as a family member of a Hanged of the Kingdom, under the Benefits for Prisoners of Zion and their Families Law 5752-1992
- A disability pension under the Nazi Persecution Invalids Law 5717-1957
- A disability pension paid by the German government under the Federal Compensation Law (BEG) 1953 to 1965
- A disability pension paid by the Netherlands under the Dutch benefits law for persecution victims 1940-1945 (WUV)
- A disability pension paid by Austria under the Victims Support Law (OFG), in the context of section 4 KOVG 57
- A disability pension paid by Belgium under its 1954 law on injury pensions for civilian war victims 1940-1945

For the last four, entitlement must be proven to the local authority's satisfaction. A survivor who is separately a Nazi-war invalid under the 5714-1954 Law gets the two-thirds ENTITLEMENT at Reg. 14e(2)(b) instead, which is the better route where both fit

**Required documents**:
- Holocaust survivor recognition documentation
- Benefit payment confirmation
- Teudat zehut

### 11. Additional Municipal Categories

Named national categories not covered above:
- **Blind person's certificate (Reg. 2(a)(5))**: ceiling up to 90%, no area cap stated.
- **Oleh dependent on the help of others (Reg. 2(a)(6a))**: ceiling up to 80% for an oleh certified by Bituach Leumi as entitled to a special benefit or a nursing benefit for olim.
- **SLA member (Reg. 2(a)(6b))**: ceiling up to 90% on 100 sqm for 12 months within 36 from arrival in Israel after May 2000, for a member recognised as rehabilitation-eligible by the MANBAS, and their spouse.
- **Nursing benefit, gimlat siud (Reg. 2(a)(7)(c))**: ceiling up to 70%.
- **Righteous Among the Nations (Reg. 2(a)(9))**: ceiling up to 66% for a person recognised by Yad Vashem, and their spouse or former spouse, resident in Israel.
- **Parent of a child entitled to the disabled-child benefit, including a foster child (Reg. 2(a)(11))**: ceiling up to 33% on 100 sqm, computed per entitled child with a combined ceiling of 90% (Arrangements Law s.12(g)).
- **Released captive (Reg. 2(a)(12))**: ceiling up to 20% for a person entitled under the Payments to Released Captives Law 5765-2005.
- **Active reserve soldier (Reg. 3f)**: ceiling up to 5% for a holder of a valid active-reservist certificate or IDF confirmation.
- **Active reserve COMMANDER (Reg. 3g)**: ceiling up to 25% on 100 sqm, for a reservist in a command role as defined in army orders, holding a valid certificate or IDF confirmation, or notified to the municipality by the IDF. This is five times the ordinary reservist ceiling and is commonly missed.
- **Hostage or missing person (Reg. 14e1)**: 100% ENTITLEMENT for the period the holder is so classified under the Hostages and Missing Persons Law 5784-2023.
- **Gaza-envelope localities (Reg. 3c)**: 45% ENTITLEMENT on residential and 39% on other property, fiscal years 2015 to 2026, for Sderot, the Eshkol, Hof Ashkelon, Sdot Negev and Sha'ar HaNegev regional councils, the Gaza-envelope localities in the Third Schedule, and any property within 7 km of the Gaza perimeter fence. State-held property is excluded.
- **Evacuated locality, Iron Swords (Reg. 3c1)**: 100% ENTITLEMENT from 7 October 2023 until the end of the evacuation or refresh period, for localities listed under the Iron Swords deferral-of-dates law. Where the government ordered only partial evacuation, only holders the municipality determined were eligible qualify.
- **Senior business owner (Reg. 14c)**: the discounts committee may extend the holder's own home income-test discount rate to the first 40 sqm of their business, where the business is their only one, is at most 75 sqm, they are 65 (60 for a woman), their turnover across all businesses does not exceed 240,000 NIS index-linked to the CPI known at the start of each fiscal year (327,191.24 NIS for 2025, per the regulation's own note), and they already receive a Reg. 2(8) discount at home.
- **Shmita year agricultural land (Reg. 3d)**: 90% to 100% for agricultural land left unworked for shmita observance for at least eight months of the fiscal year, where agricultural use is proven in two of the three preceding years.
- **New industry (Reg. 14)**: a ceiling from 10% to 75% for a new industrial plant, by year of holding and by the local unemployment rate as published by the Employment Service.

Other named national categories:
- **Income support (havtachat hachnasa) or BL maintenance (mezonot) recipient who started before 2003**: up to 70%, only where the benefit started before 2003 with no break of 6 consecutive months since. These grandfathered rows sit in a 2003 transitional provision, so they do not appear in the consolidated Reg. 2 (Kol Zchut). A later recipient's route is the Reg. 2(a)(8) income test.
- **Prisoner of Zion on an income-based benefit**: 100%, MANDATORY, on 100 sqm, where income support was paid for at least 6 months just before the claim for the income-based benefit. Otherwise the Reg. 2(a)(4) ceiling of up to 66% applies (Kol Zchut, citing the Benefits for Prisoners of Zion Law).
- **Hardship (nazak)**: up to 70% granted at the discretion of the local discounts (nazak) committee after an unexpected event causing serious income loss (illness, death in the family, sudden unemployment), or exceptionally high medical-treatment expenses. Apply with documentation of the event and the income drop.

Municipality-administered discounts and other routes:
- **Reservist soldiers (miluimnikim)**: the national rows are Reg. 3f (up to 5%) and Reg. 3g (up to 25% on 100 sqm for a commander). No enacted law giving reservists more had been found as of October 2026; bills proposing it are not law.
- **Empty properties**: the ladder is national, at Reg. 13(a), and cumulative over one person's ownership of the building, counted from the regulation's commencement on 1 March 2005: up to 100% for the first 6 months, up to 66.66% for months 7 to 12, up to 50% for months 13 to 36. A continuous vacancy shorter than 30 days does not count toward the cumulative period (Reg. 13(d)), and the holder must notify the authority 7 days before the property is used again or the last stretch may be cancelled (Reg. 13(e)). Separately, the FIRST owner of a NEW empty building unused since completion may get up to 100% for up to twelve months (Reg. 12), subject to Reg. 13(3).
- **Destroyed or damaged building (Municipalities Ordinance s.330)**: where a building is destroyed, or damaged so it cannot be lived in, and nobody lives in it, the holder gives the municipality written notice. From the notice there is no arnona for three years, then five years at the minimum rate for the property type of its last use. Nothing runs before the notice, so send it at once.
- **Homes damaged in the war with Iran (operation "Am KeLavi")**: municipalities exempt a home the municipal engineer certified as unfit to live in, whose residents were evacuated, automatically and without an application (Kol Zchut). Kol Zchut states the arrangement covers that operation only and has not been extended to "Sha'agat HaAri"; check with the municipality.
- **Multi-property discount**: NOT available. Each property is assessed independently.

## Application Process

### General Steps

1. **Determine eligibility**: Review the categories above and identify the applicable discount
2. **Gather documents**: Collect all required documentation (originals + copies)
3. **Visit or apply online**: Go to the municipality's arnona department or apply through their website
4. **Submit application**: Fill out the discount application form (tofes bakasha le-hanacha)
5. **Wait for the decision**: the council sets its own timetable (Reg. 21)
6. **Check your bill**: Verify the discount appears on the next arnona bill

### Deadlines

- **Each council sets its own deadline (Reg. 21)**, usually early in the fiscal year. Haifa 2026, for example: by 31 March 2026, or within 90 days of the charge, or within 90 days of being recognised as eligible; later applications are heard only for special reasons.
- **Retroactive discounts** depend on each authority's own rules (Kol Zchut). There is no national rule allowing a fixed number of months.
- **Rows 2(1), 2(2) and 2(7)** need no application form (Reg. 4(a)); a holder who was missed may still apply (Reg. 4(b)).

### Combining Discounts

- **Only one discount applies** (Reg. 17(a)). Where several fit, the entitled person gets the single highest, and no discount goes to a second holder of the same property.
- A holder of two or more properties gets the discount on one property only, the higher of them (Reg. 17(b)).
- A holder for part of the fiscal year gets the discount pro rata by the months held (Reg. 17(c)).
- Reg. 17(a1) is the one stacking rule: where entitlement exists BOTH under these regulations and under regulations made under s.12(a) of the Arrangements Law 5753-1992, the council MAY grant them cumulatively.
- A discount is conditional on the year's arnona balance being settled in one advance payment, by standing order, or under another arrangement the authority accepts (Reg. 20); an unpaid balance at 31 December voids it and it is added back to the debt (Reg. 16). Regs. 16, 17(b) and 20 do not apply to the Reg. 3c1 evacuation discount, and Regs. 16, 17(b), 18, 20 and 21 do not apply to Chapter Hey2.
- Since amendment 5786-2, a council may not resolve on discounts at all if the resulting revenue loss would create a current deficit in its budget; the treasurer's opinion must be laid before the council first (Reg. 22a).

### When a discount is refused

A refused discount is NOT an objection (hasaga) matter. The objection grounds in s.3(a) of the 1976 Objection Law are a closed list (zone; type, size or use; not the holder; controlling shareholder), and the arnona manager and appeals committee hear only those. So:
1. Ask the body that refused (the treasurer, or the discounts committee for Reg. 7) for the decision in writing with its reasons.
2. Answer the reasons with documents and ask for reconsideration.
3. If it stands, take advice on the administrative route.
