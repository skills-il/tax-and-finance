# Section 62A: attribution to an active shareholder

A separate statute from sections 81A to 81F, enacted by the same amendment on the same commencement
date. Run it alongside, not instead.

## Terminology

The Ordinance calls the person a controlling shareholder (baal shlita) in this section. The amending
Law replaced the earlier term. Any text using the older "substantial shareholder" wording for this
section is quoting repealed drafting.

## Classification limbs

- **Officeholder / service provider.** Turns on a holder of a quarter or more of one of the means of
  control in the OTHER body corporate, tested on any day in the tax year. Note the threshold is
  inclusive, and note the any-day test rather than a year-end snapshot.
- **Single-client dependency.** The amending Law replaced the earlier window with a shorter one
  measured over a shorter period. The percentage limb of this test was NOT amended, and could not be
  quoted from primary text during authoring; it is therefore not stated in this skill. Establish it
  from the consolidated Ordinance before relying on it.
- **Excess profitability.** The genuinely new limb, below.
- **The four-or-more-employees exclusion** was left unchanged by the amendment, and is the structural
  escape users ask about first.

## The excess-profitability attribution

```
  taxable income from labour-intensive personal-exertion activity
- 25% x ( INCOME from that activity  -  payments to a related company )
```

Two distinct income quantities appear: taxable income in the first term, gross income inside the
bracket, and the bracket is net of payments to a related company. It is NOT "the portion above a
25% margin", and a calculator built on that gloss will be wrong.

Attribution is pro rata to each active shareholder's share in the rights to the company's profits,
as personal-exertion income.

The paragraph has exactly two conditions: activity income below a ceiling that is itself multiplied
by the number of controlling shareholders, with a controlling shareholder and their relative counted
as one; and a profitability rate exceeding the stated percentage. The ceiling is a CEILING, so a
larger company falls outside this limb, which is counter-intuitive and worth stating plainly.

## The shekel figure is an exclusion, not a condition

The figure users expect as a threshold is not a condition of the attribution paragraph. It sits in
an exclusion which:

- is framed negatively, so it switches the attribution OFF rather than on;
- is gated on the company having a substantial holder; and
- offers two independent escape routes, one of which AGGREGATES accumulated profits across ALL
  companies in which that person is a substantial holder.

The aggregation limb is the operative difference. A person with several companies, each individually
below the figure but jointly above it, fails the aggregate escape while passing a naive per-company
test. Flattening the exclusion into "accumulated profits must exceed X" gives that population the
wrong answer.

The same shekel figure appears in the section 81C machinery as a shield subtracted from the base.
Same number, opposite job. Keep them apart in any explanation that covers both statutes.

## Interaction with the section 81 machinery

The one explicit, primary-sourced link runs in a single direction: profits attributed to a
shareholder and charged to tax are subtracted when computing accumulated profits under the
accounting alternative, PROVIDED those amounts were not already removed from retained earnings in
the financial statements. The proviso is the whole safeguard, and it turns on an accounting fact the
user must establish rather than on anything the skill can infer.

Whether a dividend distributed under the section 62A dividend alternative can simultaneously count
toward the section 81B safe harbours is not addressed in the primary sources. Flag it as requiring
professional advice; do not assert an answer.

## Reporting

The guidance letter sets out the company's steps in order, the certificate to each shareholder, the
withholding report and its deduction types, and new fields on the individual's annual return. It also
contains a fully-numbered official worked example. Reproduce that example rather than inventing
figures: it demonstrates that the corporate-tax credit is grossed on the attributed income while the
withholding is computed on the net dividend, which is the point implementations get wrong.
