# Transitional provisions, year by year

There is no year-varying rate table. There are two mechanisms plus one closed escape.

## Mechanism A: the annual distribution route, section 5(a)

A close company with undistributed accumulated profits on the eve of commencement day that
distributes taxed dividends at the stated rate or more of its profits remaining from commencement
day, in a year of tax years 2025 to 2030, has its excess profits for that year reduced by those
remaining profits. The rate is the same in every year it is available.

**The subsection has TWO limbs.** Limb (1) reduces the year's excess profits by the remaining
commencement-day profits. Limb (2) additionally directs that the 50% escape in section 81B(b)(2)
be read with "less the profits remaining from the profits at the end of tax year 2024" appended,
that quantity being excess profits at the end of 2024 less profits already distributed as taxed
dividends. So the DENOMINATOR of the 50% test is reduced for a company in this route. Omitting
limb (2) overstates the distribution the company needs to clear that safe harbour.

Failure consequence: a company that does not meet it in a given year is not entitled to the
reduction for that year, and the remaining commencement-day profits fall back into the charge.

## Mechanism B: the protection bands, section 5(b)

These are NOT annual rates. They are measured once, on a distribution both paid by the company and
received by the recipient inside the Determining Period, which ended on 30 November 2025. Meeting a
band extends the section 5(a) treatment into later years without a further distribution.

| Distributed in the Determining Period | Provisions apply in |
|---|---|
| Between 35% and 60% | 2026 |
| Between 60% and 75% | 2026 and 2027 |
| Between 75% and 90% | 2026 to 2028 |
| 90% or more | 2026 to 2029 |

**Every band reaches 2026.** A company that met even the lowest band is covered for 2026 without
distributing again. For a reader in 2026 this is the single most useful fact in the whole
transitional scheme.

**Boundary ambiguity.** The Law drafts these as open ranges, "between X and Y", with no
tie-breaking rule at the shared boundaries. A distribution landing exactly on one is not resolved
on the face of the Law. Say so and route it out; do not pick a side.

## Mechanism C: the 2025-only escape, section 5(c)

A close company was not liable to the addition in 2025 where the taxed dividends it distributed to
shareholders inside the Determining Period met the stated rate against accumulated profits at the
end of the preceding tax year, and provided the tax on the distribution was paid by the distributing
company by 31 December 2025.

Two conditions, not one. This is a distinct provision from the standing dividend alternative under
section 81B(b)(3), which remains available every year. They are NOT unrelated: both are measured on
accumulated profits at the end of the preceding tax year and both require taxed dividends. They
differ in rate, in window (the Determining Period against the tax year), and in section 5(c)'s
payment deadline. Sources describing the relief as one rate that simply changed into another between
2025 and 2026 misstate the structure: one is a closed transitional escape, the other a standing
alternative, and neither replaced the other.

## Year-by-year summary

- 2025: the charge applies. The three standing escapes, the annual route, and the 2025-only escape
  are all available. Bands are earned this year, not spent.
- 2026: the charge applies. The three standing escapes and the annual route are available, and any
  company that met any band in 2025 is covered automatically.
- 2027: as 2026, but only the upper three bands still reach it.
- 2028: band coverage narrows again.
- 2029: only the highest band still reaches it.
- 2030: the last year the annual route is available. No band reaches it.
- Beyond: not established in the sources. Only the standing escapes remain on the face of the Law.

The charge rate itself does not vary by year in any primary source, and neither do the standing
escape percentages.

## The closed liquidation route

A transitional route involving dissolution existed and its statutory window closed at the end of
tax year 2025. Explain it as history. It is a corporate-law decision for counsel in any event.
