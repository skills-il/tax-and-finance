# Nekudot Zikui (Tax Credit Points), full schedule

Value of one credit point, 2026: **242 NIS/month = 2,904 NIS/year** (ITA 2026 deductions
booklet, chapter ג, "נקודת זיכוי 33א").

Credit points reduce the tax liability itself, not taxable income. They cannot take the
liability below zero (no refund is generated by points alone).

Claimed by an employee on **Form 101** (part ח) and reconciled on the annual return.

## Base credits

| Who | Points | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Israeli resident | 2.25 | s.34 (2 points) + s.36 travel credit (1/4 point) |
| Woman, additional | 0.5 | s.36א |
| Resident aged 16-17 in the tax year | 1.0 | s.40ב, Form 101 box 13 |
| Spouse whose keep is on the filer (only where filer or spouse is at retirement age, blind, or disabled under s.9(5)) | 1.0 | s.37, Form 101 box 5 |

## Children, by the child's age IN the tax year

This is the schedule Form 101 part ח boxes 7 and 8 collect, and it has **six distinct age
bands**, not three. Both parents get the same points up to age 5; from age 6 the mother's
entitlement is double the father's, and the half-point in the year the child turns 18 is the
mother's only.

| Child's age in the tax year | Mother | Father |
|---|---|---|
| Year of birth | 2.5 | 2.5 |
| Ages 1-2 (from the tax year after birth to the year the child turns 2) | 4.5 | 4.5 |
| Age 3 | 3.5 | 3.5 |
| Ages 4-5 | 2.5 | 2.5 |
| Ages 6-17 (to the tax year before the year of majority) | 2.0 | 1.0 |
| Year the child turns 18 ("shnat bagrut") | 0.5 | 0 |

Source: Income Tax Ordinance s.66(ג)(4) (mother) and s.66(ג)(5) (father); the mirror schedule
for a single-parent family is s.40(ב)(1) and (1א).

**"Mother" here means the parent entitled under s.66(ג)(4)**, which in practice is the parent
receiving kitzvat yeladim from Bituach Leumi. Form 101 box 7 is filled by a single parent who
receives the child allowance, by a married woman, or by a hore yachid; box 8 by the other
parent.

Related child items:

| Item | Points / relief | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Single parent (hore be'mishpacha chad-horit), additional | 1.0 | s.40(ב)(2), Form 101 box 6 |
| Only-parent family (hore yachid), additional | 1.0 | s.40(ב)(1ב), Form 101 box 9 |
| Parent living apart who does not get the child points but contributes to the child's keep | up to 1.0, apportioned by share of the keep | s.40(ב)(2), Form 101 box 10 |
| Child who is paralysed, blind, or has an intellectual-developmental disability ("netul yecholet") | **2.0 per child**, on top of the age-band points | s.45(א), Form 101 box 11 |
| Divorced filer who remarried and pays mezonot to the former spouse | 1.0 | s.40א, Form 101 box 12 |

**s.45(א) and s.44 are mutually exclusive.** A filer who took the s.44 credit for the same
child cannot also take the s.45(א) points (s.45(ג)).

## Institution-maintenance credit (Section 44)

Not a credit point, a percentage credit. Where the filer or their spouse paid in the tax year
for maintaining a **child, spouse, or parent** in a special institution, and that person is
completely paralysed, permanently bedridden, blind, or not of sound mind (and for a child with
an intellectual-developmental disability in a special institution), the filer gets a tax credit
of **35% of the part of the sums paid that exceeds 12.5% of their taxable income** (s.44).

## New immigrant (oleh), Section 35

Entitlement runs from the **aliya date**, over a 54-month window, not by calendar year:

| Period from aliya | Rate in the statute | Points per full year in that period |
|---|---|---|
| Months 1-12 | 1/12 point per month | 1.0 |
| Months 13-30 (the next 18) | 1/4 point per month | 3.0 |
| Months 31-42 (the next 12) | 1/6 point per month | 2.0 |
| Months 43-54 (the next 12) | 1/12 point per month | 1.0 |

Total across the whole window: **8.5 points** (1 + 4.5 + 2 + 1). Source: s.35(א)(1), (1א), (2),
(3).

- The credit is given for any tax year wholly or partly inside the 54 months, pro-rated by the
  number of months the oleh sat in Israel that year (s.35(ג)).
- It is given **only on the first occasion** a person becomes an oleh (s.35(ג)).
- On request, a continuous absence from Israel of not less than 6 months and not more than 3
  years is not counted into the 54 months (s.35(ג)).
- Form 101 box 4; an aliya certificate (teudat oleh) must be attached. Where the entitlement
  period is broken by compulsory IDF service, post-secondary study, or going abroad, Form 101
  directs the employee to the pakid shuma.

**For a return whose tax year predates the current schedule, confirm the schedule that was in
force for that tax year against the Ordinance version applicable to it.** Entitlement is keyed
to the aliya date, so an older cohort can still be inside its window; do not assume the current
table governs a historic aliya date without checking.

## Returning resident (toshav chozer) under Section 35

**A returning resident is NOT generally treated as an oleh for credit points.** s.35(ד) defines
"toshav chozer" for this section narrowly: an individual who **again became an Israeli resident
between 16 May 2010 and 30 September 2012**, after having been a foreign resident for six
consecutive years. That is a closed historical cohort.

A person returning to Israel today does not get s.35 credit points on that basis. They may
still have other reliefs (for example the toshav chozer vatik exemptions, and the temporary
provision below), which are a different mechanism from nekudot zikui. Do not tell a returnee
they get "the same points as an oleh".

## Temporary provision: aliya and return incentive, in force 31.3.2026

Economic Efficiency Law 2026, chapter ד, "חוק עידוד עלייה לישראל וחזרה אליה" (hora'at sha'a),
in force 31 March 2026. This is an **exemption**, not credit points, and it is given **in
addition** to any other relief an oleh or toshav chozer vatik has.

- Who: an oleh chadash or a toshav chozer vatik who **arrived in Israel between 5 November 2025
  and 31 December 2026**.
- What: exemption from tax on income from personal exertion (yegia ishit) produced in Israel,
  meaning business and salary income, up to an annual ceiling.
- Ceilings, per s.2(א) of the hora'at sha'a, over five tax years:

| Tax year | Exemption ceiling (NIS) |
|---|---|
| 2026 | 600,000 |
| 2027 | 1,000,000 |
| 2028 | 1,000,000 |
| 2029 | 350,000 |
| 2030 | 150,000 |

As of the 2026 deductions booklet the ITA had not yet published the implementing circular
("חוזר שבו יפורטו כלל התנאים והסוגיות הנוגעות ליישום החוק יפורסם בהמשך"). Check for it before
relying on the detailed conditions.

## Discharged soldier and national service, Section 39א

Missing from most summaries and worth a full year of points. A **chayal meshuchrar** gets a
fraction of a point for **each of the 36 months following the month regular service ended**:

| Length of regular service | Per month | Points per full year | Over the whole 36 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| Man: 23 full months or more; woman: 22 full months or more | 1/6 point | 2.0 | 6.0 |
| Man: under 23 full months; woman: under 22 full months | 1/12 point | 1.0 | 3.0 |

Source: s.39א(1) and (2). "Chayal meshuchrar" and "sherut sadir" take their meaning from the
Absorption of Discharged Soldiers Law, 1994. Form 101 box 14 covers both a discharged soldier
and someone who served **sherut leumi**, and requires a copy of the discharge / service
completion certificate. The credit applies against income from personal exertion only.

## Combat reserve service, Section 39B

Added by Amendment 283 to the Ordinance, published 23 November 2025 (Sefer HaChukim 3461), in
force 1 January 2026. Detailed in ITA employers circular 2025-001368 of 16 December 2025.

**Two things trip people up.** First, the points are for miluim served in the **preceding** tax
year, not the year being filed. Second, there are **two different band tables**, and which one
applies depends on the tax year.

**Tax years 2026 and 2027 (hora'at sha'a, 1.1.2026 to 31.12.2027):**

| Combat reserve days in the preceding tax year | Points |
|---|---|
| 30-39 | 0.50 |
| 40-49 | 0.75 |
| 50-54 | 1.00 |
| 55-59 | 1.25 |
| 60-64 | 1.50 |
| 65-69 | 1.75 |
| 70-74 | 2.00 |
| 75-79 | 2.25 |
| 80-84 | 2.50 |
| 85-89 | 2.75 |
| 90-94 | 3.00 |
| 95-99 | 3.25 |
| 100-104 | 3.50 |
| 105-109 | 3.75 |
| 110 and above | 4.00 |

Below 30 days: no points under this table.

**Tax year 2028 onward (the permanent s.39B rule):** 0.75 point from **20** days, then a
further 0.25 point for each additional 5 days, capped at 4 points.

| Combat reserve days in the preceding tax year | Points |
|---|---|
| 20-24 | 0.75 |
| 25-29 | 1.00 |
| 30-34 | 1.25 |
| 35-39 | 1.50 |
| 40-44 | 1.75 |
| 45-49 | 2.00 |
| 50-54 | 2.25 |
| 55-59 | 2.50 |
| 60-64 | 2.75 |
| 65-69 | 3.00 |
| 70-74 | 3.25 |
| 75-79 | 3.50 |
| 80-84 | 3.75 |
| 85 and above | 4.00 |

Both tables cap at **4 points**, which at the 2026 point value is 11,616 NIS of tax. A cap of
1.0 point understates a heavy reservist's entitlement by up to 8,712 NIS.

- "Lochem" is defined by army orders under the Military Justice Law, 1955; "sherut miluim" by
  the Reserve Service Law, 2008.
- An **IDF certificate** of entitlement for combat reserve service must be attached, and the
  points are given on that certificate alone (circular s.6). The IDF certificate itself states
  the day count is not final and may change.
- Form 101 collects the day count at part ח, box 16.

## Education, Section 40ג

| Qualification | Points | For how many tax years |
|---|---|---|
| First academic degree | 1.0 | As many tax years as the degree took, capped at 3 |
| Second academic degree | 0.5 | As many tax years as the degree took, capped at 2 |
| Third degree in medicine or dentistry | 1.0 for 3 years, then 0.5 for 2 years | See s.40ג(ד)(1) |
| Vocational studies certificate (limudei miktzoa) | 1.0 | Form 101 box 15 collects it alongside the academic credit. s.40ג itself speaks of academic degrees, so confirm the year count for vocational studies against the current ITA guidance before quoting it |

Points start in the tax year **after** the tax year studies ended. Where the field requires an
internship (hitmachut) whose completion is a condition of the profession, the filer may elect
to start the points in the tax year after the internship ended instead (s.40ג(ג1)). Declared on
Form 119, referenced by Form 101 box 15.

## Section 11: yishuv mutav relief (a discount, not credit points)

s.11 gives a **percentage discount on tax** on income from personal exertion only, for a
permanent resident of a qualifying yishuv, capped at an annual income ceiling. The rate and the
ceiling are **per yishuv**, not uniform, and the list changes every year.

The authoritative list for 2026 is chapter ח of the ITA 2026 deductions booklet. Do not quote a
rate from memory; read the row for the specific yishuv. Changes flagged in the 2026 booklet:

- Kedem Arava added, 10%, ceiling 180,000 NIS; Yonadav added, 7%, ceiling 146,640 NIS; Bitron
  added, 12%, ceiling 213,240 NIS.
- Hatavot Mas LeYishuvim Law (hora'at sha'a) for 2026-2029 changed rates and ceilings for part
  of the Chevel Tekuma yishuvim and added the city of Ashkelon to the list.
- Encouragement of Mixed Urban Yishuv Law (hora'at sha'a) 2026-2029: someone resident all tax
  year in a qualifying mixed urban yishuv gets a **12% credit up to an income ceiling of
  226,560 NIS**. Nof HaGalil was added under this provision.

Form 101 box 3, with a Form 1312א certificate from the local authority.
